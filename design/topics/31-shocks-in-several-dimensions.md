# Shocks in several dimensions

## 1. Overview

In several dimensions shocks meet walls, corners, each other and dense clouds, which tests the multidimensional integrator, the symmetry of the scheme and its robustness at very high Mach numbers, besides the capture of the shocks themselves. Blast waves with self-similar solutions test geometry and symmetry, converging shocks test robustness, reflections from walls and steps test the diffusion of contacts and the noise behind strong shocks, and clouds in a wind or struck by a shock test the destruction of dense structures. Zingale's text, pyro and Ulula run blast waves, Zingale's text and pyro also two-dimensional Riemann problems, pyro the double Mach reflection, Ulula the crushing of a cloud, ZEUS-2D the two-dimensional problems of Woodward and Colella, and Athena and Athena++ the double Mach reflection, an implosion, the problem of Noh and a shocked cloud. The one-dimensional problems follow in [topic 30](30-shock-tubes-and-interacting-blast-waves.md), and the Kelvin–Helmholtz and Rayleigh–Taylor instabilities in [topic 32](32-instabilities-vortices-and-convection.md).

## 2. Problems

### 2.1 The Sedov–Taylor blast wave

An energy deposited at a point of a uniform medium drives a spherical or cylindrical shock that evacuates its centre, with the self-similar solution of [Sedov](#sedov-1959) (1959) ([Zingale's text](#zingale-2025), §8.9.2). Energy in a single cell would change its extent with resolution and be squared off on a Cartesian grid, so that Zingale's text, after [Omang, Børve and Trulsen](#omang-et-al-2006) (2006), inflates a small region with the corresponding pressure, pyro averages sub-cells near its edge, and Ulula spreads the energy over a Gaussian of 0.75 cell widths. The problem tests the geometric factors of one-dimensional spherical and two-dimensional axisymmetric grids, and in Cartesian coordinates the symmetry of the scheme. Ulula compares with an exact solution computed for one, two or three dimensions, and Athena++ runs blasts in curvilinear coordinates ([topic 12, §3](12-curvilinear-coordinates.md#3-tests)).

Detail: [Zingale 2.5.19](../references/computational-hydrodynamics-synthesis.md#2519-test-problems), [pyro 2.4.12](../references/pyro-synthesis.md#2412-problems-and-tests), [Ulula 2.8.11](../references/ulula-synthesis.md#2811-the-sedovtaylor-explosion), [Athena 2.8.7](../references/athena-synthesis.md#287-tests-in-curvilinear-coordinates).

### 2.2 The problem of Noh

In the problem of [Noh](#noh-1987) (1987), cold gas falls radially onto the origin at unit speed and forms a shock of formally infinite Mach number that moves out at 1/3, behind which the gas of the spherical version is uniform at a density of 64 for $\gamma = 5/3$. [Stone et al.](#stone-et-al-2008) (2008, §8.5) run the spherical version, probably the hardest on a Cartesian grid, on $200^3$ cells of the positive octant with reflecting inner boundaries, a tiny pressure in place of zero, and outer boundaries that follow the analytic inflow. The problem tests robustness at extreme Mach numbers and the noise along the directions of the grid.

Detail: [Athena 2.16.5](../references/athena-synthesis.md#2165-hydrodynamics-in-two-and-three-dimensions).

### 2.3 The implosion

The implosion of [Liska and Wendroff](#liska-wendroff-2003) (2003, §4.7), after [Hui et al.](#hui-et-al-1999) (1999), separates the states of Sod's problem, as Stone et al. describe them, by a discontinuity at $45^\circ$ near a corner of a square with reflecting walls. A shock runs into the corner, jets along the two walls collide there, and vortices travel out along the diagonal while reflected shocks drive the Richtmyer–Meshkov instability. The jet runs exactly along the diagonal only if the scheme keeps the reflective symmetry across it to machine precision, which makes the problem an extraordinarily sensitive test of symmetry. Athena runs it with third order and HLLC, and Athena++ with PPM in characteristic variables, HLLC and RK3 on $512^2$ cells, also with adaptive refinement ([Stone et al.](#stone-et-al-2020) 2020, §3.3.5).

Detail: [Athena 2.16.5](../references/athena-synthesis.md#2165-hydrodynamics-in-two-and-three-dimensions).

### 2.4 The double Mach reflection

In the double Mach reflection of [Woodward and Colella](#woodward-colella-1984) (1984), a shock of Mach 10 in a gas with $\gamma = 1.4$ meets a wall at $60^\circ$, set up obliquely on a rectangular grid so that it reflects from the lower boundary. The incident and reflected shocks meet in a triple point, from which a contact leaves, and a short jet forms along the wall, whose structure depends strongly on how much a scheme diffuses contacts. The upper boundary must follow the exact motion of the incident shock. ZEUS-2D runs it on three grids to $t = 0.2$ ([Stone and Norman](#stone-norman-1992a) 1992a, §5.5.3), Athena with reconstruction of second and third order, pyro on $1024 \times 256$ cells, and Athena++ also with adaptive refinement.

Detail: [pyro 2.4.12](../references/pyro-synthesis.md#2412-problems-and-tests), [ZEUS 2.7.4](../references/zeus-synthesis.md#274-strong-shocks), [Athena 2.16.5](../references/athena-synthesis.md#2165-hydrodynamics-in-two-and-three-dimensions).

### 2.5 The wind tunnel with a step

A flow at Mach 3 enters a tunnel 3 long and 1 high with a step 0.2 high, reflects from the step and the walls, and forms a pattern of shocks with a Mach stem (Stone and Norman 1992a, §5.5.2). The corner of the step is a singular point, whose numerical errors Woodward and Colella reduce by resetting density and speed in a few cells next to it, which on a staggered mesh is hard to do properly. ZEUS-2D runs it on three grids to $t = 4$ with the van Leer method and PPA and reports the cost of each run.

Detail: [ZEUS 2.7.4](../references/zeus-synthesis.md#274-strong-shocks).

### 2.6 Two-dimensional Riemann problems

Four constant states in the quadrants about a corner, after [Schulz-Rinne et al.](#schulz-rinne-et-al-1993) (1993) and [Lax and Liu](#lax-liu-1998) (1998), launch waves whose interaction tests the symmetry of a code in complex flow (Zingale's text, §8.9.5). Zingale's text shows configuration 3 at $t = 0.8$, and pyro runs one such configuration on $256 \times 256$ cells with outflow boundaries. Its problem `logo` lets the shocks of explosions in the four corners converge on a word drawn in density.

Detail: [Zingale 2.5.19](../references/computational-hydrodynamics-synthesis.md#2519-test-problems), [pyro 2.4.12](../references/pyro-synthesis.md#2412-problems-and-tests).

### 2.7 Clouds in a wind or a shock

Ulula crushes a cloud of density 100 and radius 0.02 in a wind of density 1 moving at 0.1, on $300 \times 150$ cells, with a boundary that holds the wind on the inflow side; it cites [Klein, McKee and Colella](#klein-et-al-1994) (1994) for the problem and notes that its outcome depends on the hydrodynamic method and the physics included ([Diemer](#diemer-2025) 2025, §4.1). Athena++ drives a planar shock of Mach 10 into a cloud ten times denser than its surroundings on $160 \times 80$ cells, optionally with a field along the flow. Both test the destruction of a dense structure, which in Ulula proceeds at a rate that depends strongly on resolution. The shock–cloud problem of Klein, McKee and Colella and its successors with cooling, conduction and fields follow in topic 34.

Detail: [Ulula 2.8.10](../references/ulula-synthesis.md#2810-cloud-crushing), [Athena 2.16.5](../references/athena-synthesis.md#2165-hydrodynamics-in-two-and-three-dimensions).

## 3. Assessment

In one-dimensional spherical geometry the Sedov blast of Zingale's text matches the analytic shock position, density and velocity, with a pressure slightly low behind the shock, likely from the initialisation; in two Cartesian dimensions density and pressure look symmetric, but the speed and the internal energy carry an imprint of the grid along the axes, and the internal energy a bump near the origin from the error in computing it from the total energy and the velocity, while the angle-averaged profile follows the solution. Ulula reports that it reproduces the analytic solution, and its radial profiles follow it except at the shock, where the peak density reaches 3.3 against the jump to 4 and the pressure 80 against 87. Athena handles the problem of Noh with smooth spherical contours, the right jump and speed and little scatter, apart from the wall heating at the origin, a problem whose cylindrical version few codes of Liska and Wendroff managed at all; without the H-correction the shock distorts along the grid, and below about $50^3$ cells Roe's solver can fail unless the Courant number is lowered (Stone et al. 2008). The implosion of the unsplit CTU forms the jet along the diagonal, which split schemes lose, and in Athena++ the solution stays symmetric to double precision, provided stencils are written without directional bias and neither MPI nor the compiler gives up value-safe arithmetic, so that operations are neither reassociated nor contracted into fused multiply–adds (Stone et al. 2020). In the double Mach reflection and the wind tunnel, resolution matters greatly, PPA beats the van Leer method at every resolution at about twice the cost, and the error from the corner of the step, a spurious Mach stem, shifts the shocks so that only the finest grids come close to the solution of Woodward and Colella (Stone and Norman 1992a); on $260 \times 80$ cells Athena's jet differs slightly but visibly between second and third order, the latter being a little less diffusive, and the H-correction removes small noise behind the shock in every run. Ulula's cloud is pressed into a crescent, stretched and carried downstream, its density falling from 100 to about 6 with a reversed flow behind it. **[GK]** On the finest grids, ZEUS-2D updated about $2.2 \times 10^5$ zones per second with the van Leer method on a Cray X-MP, a first yardstick for the rate of cell updates. Ulula's wind is subsonic, at Mach 0.08, whereas Klein, McKee and Colella treat mainly clouds struck by shocks, with one variant in a wind without a shock ([topic 34, §2.1](34-cold-clouds-and-mixing-layers.md#21-the-shockcloud-problem)); pyro's quadrant problem differs from configuration 3 of Lax and Liu in one density, its double Mach reflection samples its initial cells outside them, and its logo fails with current versions of Matplotlib, while the input of the implosion in Athena++ sets the pressure 0.14 inside the corner, not the 0.1 of Sod's problem. The blast waves separate schemes by symmetry and geometry, the problem of Noh by robustness, the double Mach reflection by the diffusion of contacts and the noise behind strong shocks, and the implosion by exact symmetry. The clouds come closest to the production runs, a cloud moving through a hot corona, where cooling, conduction and resolution decide its fate rather than the hydrodynamic scheme alone; a cloud in a subsonic wind, as in Ulula, serves them better than a shocked one ([topic 34, §2.6](34-cold-clouds-and-mixing-layers.md#26-fountain-clouds-through-a-corona)).
