# B7 The check of docstrings

**Status.** Decided on 2026-11-21.

**Topics.** None, since no reference of `reference-works/` treats the docstrings of its code.

**Supersedes.** None.

## Scope

The brief settles how the docstrings of mhdpy are kept in line with the code that they describe. Outside the tests, §3.2 of [CLAUDE.md](../../CLAUDE.md) gives each docstring the sections Parameters, Returns and Raises, those that apply, each entry with its type written as in the annotations and its default in brackets, as `mass : float (default = 1.0)`. A type copied by hand drifts from its annotation as soon as one of them changes, and pyright, which checks the annotations, reads no docstring. The brief decides what checks the docstrings and what the check compares. The form itself is the one of §3.2, which the brief takes as given.

The brief builds on [B1D5](B01-strata-in-package.md#b1d5-the-check-of-the-rule) and [B6D7](B06-testing-and-verification.md#b6d7-the-check-of-requirements), whose checks run as tests at the top of `tests/` in every full run, and so in every commit, and on [B4D4](B04-packaging.md#b4d4-the-dependencies), under which the tools of development lie in groups of dependencies, locked in `uv.lock`, and never weigh on the installation.

- [B7D1](#b7d1-the-tool-of-the-check) The tool of the check: decided.
- [B7D2](#b7d2-what-the-check-compares) What the check compares: decided.

## B7D1 The tool of the check

**Decision.** In the context of docstrings whose types and defaults are copied by hand from the code, facing types that drift from their annotations unseen by pyright, a test of its own at the top of `tests/`, which reads the source with `ast` and compares each documented type with its annotation after B6D7 (option A), is chosen over pydoclint (B). This achieves readability, as the check reads the form of §3.2 as decided and takes the shape of the check of requirements, accepting a module and its tests to maintain. Decided on 2026-11-21, as recommended.

**Question.** Whether the docstrings are checked by code of mhdpy or by a tool of development decides whether the form of §3.2 stays as it is, since a tool reads only the forms that it knows. It must be settled before the scope of the check, as each tool brings its own range of checks, and before the requirements of the check are written. The docstrings revised to the form of §3.2 lie in `src/mhdpy/infrastructure/provenance.py`, `src/mhdpy/infrastructure/units.py` and `tests/test_requirements.py`, the code of the check of requirements. To be read first are B6D7, whose check is a model for option A, and B1D5, which chose a tool of development over a test of its own.

**Option A: a test of its own, after B6D7.** A module at the top of `tests/` reads the source of every module with the module `ast` of the standard library, without importing it, finds the sections of each docstring by their titles and rules of `=`, and compares the type of each entry with the text of its annotation, which `ast.unparse` gives. It gathers every fault before it fails, as the check of requirements does. **[GK]** For mhdpy, the check reads exactly the form of §3.2, the fields of a dataclass or named tuple in the Parameters of its class among them, whose annotations stand on the class rather than on a function, and needs no dependency. It is code that the author writes and maintains, with tests of its own on small trees of modules, as [§3.1](../overview.md#31-infrastructure) of the overview asks of every tool of testing, and it checks only what its requirements name. A change of the form in §3.2 changes the check with it.

**Option B: pydoclint, a tool of development.** **[GK]** pydoclint reads the source with `ast`, without importing it, and compares the types of the docstrings in the style of numpydoc with the annotations, as text and verbatim, for the parameters (DOC105), the return value (DOC203) and the attributes of a class (DOC605) ([pydoclint](#pydoclint)). Its parser recognises a section only by a title underlined by dashes of exactly its length ([docstring_parser_fork](#docstring-parser-fork)), so that the rules of `=` of §3.2 hide every section from it. It reads a default only in the forms `, default=1.0` or `(default)`, and would take `float (default = 1.0)` whole for a type. It expects the fields of a class under Attributes rather than Parameters ([pydoclint](#pydoclint)). Run without its cache by a test at the top of `tests/`, as B1D5 runs Import Linter, it would fail every commit on a fault. For mhdpy, it brings a range of checks maintained by others, the names, order and defaults of the parameters and the exceptions raised among them, for two packages of development, click and a fork of docstring_parser pinned to one version, locked under B4D4. It demands a new form in §3.2, with dashes under each title, the default after a comma and the fields under Attributes, and the revision of every docstring of `d68458a` once more. A checker of numpydoc itself is no option, since its validation compares the names and order of the parameters but not their types, and imports the module to read them ([numpydoc](#numpydoc)).

**Experiments.** None, since the documentation of pydoclint and the source of its parser settle which forms it reads, and the form of §3.2 is known.

**Recommendation.** Option A, since it checks the form that §3.2 sets, which the author chose one docstring at a time, rather than a form that a tool imposes. pydoclint compares types verbatim, as a test of its own would, so that correctness, the first priority of §1, is served alike by both for the types. Its further checks would need a scope of their own in either case, which the next sub-decision decides. A test of its own takes the shape of the check of requirements, reads the source as that check does and needs no dependency, so that the author can read every line of what judges the docstrings. Unlike the rule between the strata of B1D5, which Import Linter reads as it is, the form here is one that no tool reads, and it is small and fixed by §3.2. The option serves readability, the third priority of §1, and gives up none above it. Its price is a module and its tests to maintain, and checks beyond its scope that pydoclint would have brought for free.

**Set aside.** Option B would return if the form of §3.2 came to follow numpydoc, with dashes under each title and defaults after a comma, or if the check came to need many of the further checks that pydoclint already makes.

## B7D2 What the check compares

**Decision.** In the context of docstrings that repeat the signatures of their code, facing parameters added, renamed or removed without their entries, the types and defaults of every parameter and field, their names in their order, and a Returns exactly where the return annotation is other than `None` (option C) are compared, over the types alone (A), the types and defaults (B) and the form and exceptions as well (D). This achieves correctness, as a docstring that misses its signature, or a section that the check cannot find, fails, accepting a larger check to read. Decided on 2026-11-21, as recommended.

**Question.** [B7D1](#b7d1-the-tool-of-the-check) compares each documented type with its annotation, while what else the check holds the docstrings to is open. A docstring drifts from its code in several ways: a type or a default changes, a parameter is added, renamed or removed, or a function that returned nothing comes to return a value. The check sees only what it compares, and each further comparison is code to write, test and maintain. The scope must be settled before the requirements, which state each comparison as one requirement. In every option, the check covers the docstrings outside the tests, as §3.2 does, and reads the forms of §3.2 and of the docstrings revised in `d68458a`: `*arguments : str`, the fields of a dataclass with defaults inside `field(default = 1.0)`, and the properties, which keep a summary alone and so lie outside every comparison, as do the class variables.

**Option A: the types alone.** The type of each entry of Parameters equals the annotation of the parameter or field that it names, and the type of an entry of Returns equals the return annotation. An entry that names no parameter is a fault, since its type has nothing to compare with. **[GK]** For mhdpy, the check is the smallest that meets the decision of the author to compare the types. A parameter added without an entry passes unseen, as do a changed default and a missing Returns.

**Option B: the types and the defaults.** As option A, and the default of each entry equals the default in the code, while a parameter without a default shows none. **[GK]** For mhdpy, the defaults are copied by hand as the types are and drift in the same way, as when the default mass of `Units` changes from `1.0` to `1e3`. The check reads the defaults of a function from its signature, and those of a dataclass from its fields, directly or inside `field`. A parameter added without an entry still passes unseen.

**Option C: the whole signature.** As option B, and the entries of Parameters name every parameter of a function, without `self` or `cls`, or every field of a class, in their order and nothing more, while Returns stands exactly where the return annotation is other than `None`. **[GK]** For mhdpy, the most common drift, a parameter added to a function without an entry, then fails, as does a parameter renamed in the code alone. A section whose title or rule is malformed, so that the check cannot find it, fails as missing rather than passing unseen. Over option B, the check compares two lists of names and looks for one section, at little further cost.

**Option D: the signature and the form.** As option C, and the titles stand in the order Parameters, Returns, Raises, each under a rule of `=` that reaches column 120, and the entries of Raises name the exceptions that the function raises. **[GK]** For mhdpy, the order of the titles and the length of the rules are form that a reader sees at a glance. The exceptions cannot be decided from the source: `_git` in `provenance.py` raises `subprocess.CalledProcessError` through `subprocess.run` with `check = True`, without a statement `raise` of its own. A comparison with the statements `raise` would fail on correct docstrings, or need its exceptions listed by hand.

**Experiments.** None, since each option is defined by what it compares, and the docstrings of `d68458a` show the forms that it must read.

**Recommendation.** Option C, since a docstring serves only while it agrees with its code, and the drift least likely to be seen in review, a parameter added or renamed without its entry, passes options A and B. Option C also turns a section that the check cannot find into a fault, so that a malformed title cannot hide a whole docstring from the check. Its cost over option A is the reading of defaults and the comparison of two lists of names, which stay within one module. Option D adds form that the eye checks, and a comparison of exceptions that the source cannot decide. The option serves correctness, the first priority of §1, at the price of a larger check to read, the third.

**Set aside.** The comparison of exceptions of option D would return if they came to be decided from the source, as by a type checker that tracks them. Its check of the form would return if a malformed title or rule came to pass unseen, as one of Raises, which no comparison of option C requires, can.
