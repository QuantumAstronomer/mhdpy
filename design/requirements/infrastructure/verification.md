# Verification

**Prefix.** VERI.

**Status.** Agreed on 2026-11-11.

**Briefs.** [B6D1](../../briefs/B06-testing-and-verification.md#b6d1-the-place-of-the-tools), [B6D2](../../briefs/B06-testing-and-verification.md#b6d2-the-norms), [B6D3](../../briefs/B06-testing-and-verification.md#b6d3-the-order-of-convergence), [B6D4](../../briefs/B06-testing-and-verification.md#b6d4-exact-and-round-off-comparisons) and [B6D5](../../briefs/B06-testing-and-verification.md#b6d5-conservation).

## Scope

The tools of testing measure how far a result lies from its reference and how fast its error falls as the grid is refined, and judge whether two results agree exactly or to round-off and whether a total is conserved to round-off, for the tests of every stratum, the problems of stratum 5 and users alike, as [B6D1](../../briefs/B06-testing-and-verification.md#b6d1-the-place-of-the-tools) to [B6D5](../../briefs/B06-testing-and-verification.md#b6d5-conservation) decide. They are the module `mhdpy.infrastructure.verification`, with the functions `errors` and `orders` and the assertions `assert_exact`, `assert_round_off` and `assert_conserved`. The bounds of each requirement on convergence, on the order of the finest pair and on its error, are left to the requirements of each component, and the problems with their measures to the brief on the problems that verify the first pass. The record of what boundaries and sources add to a total is left to the history of the brief on outputs. As the author decided, the regression tests of [B6D6](../../briefs/B06-testing-and-verification.md#b6d6-regression-tests) join this document with their first benchmark, and the averaging of a finer grid onto a coarser one, by which [B6D3](../../briefs/B06-testing-and-verification.md#b6d3-the-order-of-convergence) measures errors where no solution is known, with the first problem that needs it.

A state is a sequence of arrays of NumPy over the same cells, one for each variable, which a state with its variables on its first axis already is, whatever the order of the axes that the brief on the arrays of the state chooses. The volumes are an array over those cells, or one number where the cells have equal volumes. A result is compared with a reference, a known solution or another version of the same computation, and the largest magnitude of a variable is its largest absolute value over its cells. Round-off is bounded in terms of $\epsilon = 2^{-52}$, the machine epsilon of double precision, and $k = 2^{12}$, and a number agrees with another to round-off where their difference is zero or below $k\epsilon$ times the magnitude of the other. A comparison of different code spans at most 64 steps, for which B6D4 fixed its bound, so that the tests that use it keep to that span. The assertions raise `AssertionError`, naming the variable and its largest difference in units of $\epsilon$, in the manner of `numpy.testing`, and a fault of their arguments raises `ValueError`. The tools rely on NumPy and on the module `math`.

## Errors

<a id="veri01"></a>**VERI01** `errors` shall give, for each variable of a result and its reference, the $L_1$ error $\sum_i |q_i - q_i^\mathrm{ref}| V_i / \sum_i V_i$ as its field `l1`, to round-off. [B6D2](../../briefs/B06-testing-and-verification.md#b6d2-the-norms) measures the error of each conserved variable by this norm, after Athena++, whose linear waves and shock tubes are measured so ([Athena 2.16.1](../../references/athena-synthesis.md#2161-linear-waves), [2.16.4](../../references/athena-synthesis.md#2164-shock-tubes)). One norm then serves the smooth problems and the shock tubes of every stratum, as it falls at first order across a jump smeared over a few cells, where norms of a higher power fall at half that order or not at all ([topic 28, §2.2](../../topics/28-verification-and-testing.md#22-norms-and-the-order-of-convergence)). Weighted by volume, it is the mean error over the domain on any grid.

<a id="veri02"></a>**VERI02** `errors` shall give as its field `state` the error of the state, the root of the sum of the squares of the $L_1$ errors of its variables. [B6D2](../../briefs/B06-testing-and-verification.md#b6d2-the-norms) combines the errors of the variables as Athena++ does, which reports this number as their root mean square without dividing by the number of variables ([Athena 2.16.1](../../references/athena-synthesis.md#2161-linear-waves)), so that the errors of mhdpy compare with those that Athena++ publishes and with the thresholds of its tests. The sum mixes quantities of different dimensions, which the code units of [B5D4](../../briefs/B05-parameters-and-units.md#b5d4-the-system-of-units) keep near unity.

<a id="veri03"></a>**VERI03** `errors` shall give as its field `maximum` the maximum error of each variable, and as its field `ratio` the largest ratio of maximum to $L_1$ error over the variables whose $L_1$ error is not zero, or zero where none is. [B6D2](../../briefs/B06-testing-and-verification.md#b6d2-the-norms) sets the maximum error and this ratio beside the norm, as the ratio shows a fault confined to a few cells, such as a wrong copy of ghost cells between blocks, which a global norm averages away. A variable without error has no ratio, and where every variable agrees exactly, no cell holds a fault to show.

## Orders

<a id="veri04"></a>**VERI04** `orders` shall give, for the errors on a sequence of grids each twice as fine as the one before, the order $\log_2(\epsilon_N/\epsilon_{2N})$ of each pair, the coarsest first. [B6D3](../../briefs/B06-testing-and-verification.md#b6d3-the-order-of-convergence) has the requirements of each component bound the order of the finest pair from below and its error from above, after Athena++, while the tools give the order of every pair, so that a test can show its trend. The finer grid of one pair serves as the coarser of the next ([topic 28, §2.3](../../topics/28-verification-and-testing.md#23-convergence-without-an-exact-solution)).

<a id="veri05"></a>**VERI05** If an error given to `orders` is not positive and finite, then `orders` shall raise `ValueError`. The logarithm of a ratio holds for positive errors alone. An error of zero, as when a test compares a result with itself, would give an infinite order that a bound from below passes, and a NaN an order without meaning.

## Comparisons

<a id="veri06"></a>**VERI06** `assert_exact` shall pass where each variable of a result is identical bit for bit to that of its reference, and fail otherwise. [B6D4](../../briefs/B06-testing-and-verification.md#b6d4-exact-and-round-off-comparisons) requires results of the same compiled code on the same data to agree exactly: runs on any number of threads ([B3D3](../../briefs/B03-parallel-execution.md#b3d3-whether-it-enters-the-first-pass)), restarts, which [§3.5 of the overview](../../overview.md#35-driver-and-problems) requires to agree bit for bit, and mirror images where the scheme is written symmetrically. Identity of bits tells a negative zero from a positive one, which an equality of values would not.

<a id="veri07"></a>**VERI07** `assert_round_off` shall pass where the largest difference of each variable between a result and its reference is zero or below $k\epsilon$ times the largest magnitude of that variable in the reference, or in the state given as `scale`, and fail otherwise. [B6D4](../../briefs/B06-testing-and-verification.md#b6d4-exact-and-round-off-comparisons) compares different code, a kernel and its reference, the versions of a kernel or its source run uncompiled, by one rule for all tests. Against the largest magnitude of each variable in the state, round-off stays below six hundred times $\epsilon$ over 64 steps, the fusions of ARM64 and a reference written apart from its loop included ([B6E1](../../experiments/B06-testing-and-verification/E1-round-off-against-references/README.md)), so that $k$ leaves a margin while a fault of the algorithm differs by orders of magnitude more. A change, such as that of one stage, is measured against the state that it changes, given as the scale, since against its own size its round-off grows with the number of cells. A variable that is zero everywhere has no magnitude to measure by and must then agree exactly, which the strict bound of B6D4 would refuse even to identical arrays.

<a id="veri08"></a>**VERI08** If a result, its reference or the scale holds a NaN or an infinity, then `assert_exact` and `assert_round_off` shall fail. [B6D4](../../briefs/B06-testing-and-verification.md#b6d4-exact-and-round-off-comparisons) lets any NaN fail a comparison of different code, and the author extended the rule to exact comparisons, as two runs that put a NaN in the same cell agree bit for bit while both are broken. An infinity fails as well, since an infinite largest magnitude would let the bound of [VERI07](#veri07) pass any difference.

<a id="veri09"></a>**VERI09** If the states that a tool takes differ in their number of variables or in the shapes of their arrays, then the tool shall raise `ValueError`. A mismatch is a fault of the test rather than a difference of results, which NumPy would otherwise broadcast into a comparison without meaning.

## Conservation

<a id="veri10"></a>**VERI10** `assert_conserved` shall pass where each variable keeps $|Q_n - Q_0 - A| \le 16\epsilon nS$ over its $n$ steps, and fail otherwise, with the totals $Q = \sum_i q_i V_i$ of the initial and final states and $S$, the larger of $\sum_i |q_i| V_i$ at the start and at the end, summed by `math.fsum`, and $A$ what boundaries and sources added, zero unless given. [B6D5](../../briefs/B06-testing-and-verification.md#b6d5-conservation) bounds the change of a total by the arithmetic rather than by a choice for each test. Each addition rounds its result by at most half of $\epsilon$, and a scheme adds to each cell a few times a step, so that round-off alone changes a total by at most a small multiple of $\epsilon nS$, and the factor 16 holds for integrators that round each cell up to some thirty times a step. $S$ stays positive where a total vanishes, as the momentum of a wave at rest does, so that every total is checked, and `math.fsum` avoids the loss of precision of a running sum. A fault that breaks conservation, such as a face whose fluxes two blocks compute from different ghost cells, changes a total by far more at every step. Where boundaries or sources add to a total, the rule applies to the change less what they added.

## Verification

<a id="veri11"></a>**VERI11** When a result differs from its reference by $C h^p$ in every cell, with $h$ halved from grid to grid and $p$ known, the orders that `orders` gives for the errors of `errors` shall equal $p$ to round-off. The tools of testing on sequences of known order close stratum 1 ([§3.1 of the overview](../../overview.md#31-infrastructure)), since tools of verification need tests as much as the code that they check ([topic 28, §4](../../topics/28-verification-and-testing.md#4-assessment)). A difference uniform over the cells makes the $L_1$ error its size, so that the order is that of the sequence alone, without the error of a discretisation.
