# Riemann solvers

## 1. Overview

At every face the reconstruction of [topic 6](06-reconstruction-and-limiting.md) leaves two states, $\mathbf{W}_\mathrm{L}$ and $\mathbf{W}_\mathrm{R}$, and the Riemann solver turns them into the flux of the conservative update of [topic 3, §2.3](03-grids-and-finite-volume-discretisation.md#23-the-conservative-update), from the waves that the jump between them launches. An average of the two fluxes would ignore that the waves cross the face in a definite direction. The exact solution of the Riemann problem of the Euler equations costs a root finding per face, so that the codes use approximate solvers: the solver of Colella, Glaz and Ferguson in pyro, the HLL family in pyro, Ulula, Athena and Athena++, with HLLC for hydrodynamics and HLLD for MHD, and the linearised solver of Roe in Athena and Athena++, while Athena also implements exact solvers for isothermal hydrodynamics and may fall back on an exact solver where Roe's fails (2.6). ZEUS needs none, as it upwinds by interpolation and adds artificial viscosity ([topic 10](10-artificial-viscosity.md)). Solvers for relativistic MHD follow in [topic 25, §2.4](25-relativistic-mhd.md#24-riemann-solvers-and-frames). Exact solvers for MHD and solvers built on other approximations are only named, by Athena.

## 2. Methods

### 2.1 The Riemann problem and its exact solution

For Burgers' equation, with the flux $f(u) = u^2/2$, the Rankine–Hugoniot condition gives the speed of a shock, $S = [f(u_\mathrm{R}) - f(u_\mathrm{L})]/(u_\mathrm{R} - u_\mathrm{L}) = \tfrac{1}{2}(u_\mathrm{L} + u_\mathrm{R})$, and a jump with $u_\mathrm{L} < u_\mathrm{R}$ opens into a rarefaction instead ([Zingale's text](#zingale-2025), eqs. 6.8 to 6.15). The Riemann problem of the Euler equations has three waves, which separate the outer states from two star states, $\mathbf{W}^*_\mathrm{L}$ and $\mathbf{W}^*_\mathrm{R}$ (eqs. 7.38 to 7.94). The middle wave is a contact, across which $v_x^*$ and $p^*$ are continuous, so that the unknowns are $p^*$, $v_x^*$, $\rho^*_\mathrm{L}$ and $\rho^*_\mathrm{R}$. Each outer wave is a shock where the flow compresses and a rarefaction otherwise.

Across a rarefaction the entropy is constant, and for a gamma-law gas $v_x + 2c_\mathrm{s}/(\gamma - 1)$ across the left wave, or $v_x - 2c_\mathrm{s}/(\gamma - 1)$ across the right one, is a Riemann invariant, while a general equation of state needs the integral itself. Across a shock the Rankine–Hugoniot conditions hold, with the mass flux through the shock relative to it, positive on both sides, $\dot M_\mathrm{L} = \rho_\mathrm{L}(v_{x,\mathrm{L}} - S_\mathrm{L})$ and $\dot M_\mathrm{R} = \rho_\mathrm{R}(S_\mathrm{R} - v_{x,\mathrm{R}})$ for the shock speeds $S_\mathrm{L}$ and $S_\mathrm{R}$. For a gamma-law gas both branches have closed forms:

$$v_x^* = v_{x,\mathrm{L}} - \begin{cases} (p^* - p_\mathrm{L})/\dot M_\mathrm{L}, & p^* > p_\mathrm{L}, \\[2pt] \dfrac{2c_{\mathrm{s,L}}}{\gamma - 1}\left[(p^*/p_\mathrm{L})^{(\gamma - 1)/2\gamma} - 1\right], & p^* \le p_\mathrm{L}, \end{cases} \qquad \dot M_\mathrm{K}^2 = \tfrac{1}{2}\rho_\mathrm{K}\left[(\gamma + 1)\,p^* + (\gamma - 1)\,p_\mathrm{K}\right],$$

and alike from the right with the opposite signs. The shock moves at $S_\mathrm{L} = v_{x,\mathrm{L}} - \dot M_\mathrm{L}/\rho_\mathrm{L}$, and the density behind it is $\rho^*_\mathrm{K} = \rho_\mathrm{K}[(\gamma + 1)p^*/p_\mathrm{K} + (\gamma - 1)]/[(\gamma + 1) + (\gamma - 1)p^*/p_\mathrm{K}]$, while a rarefaction keeps $p/\rho^\gamma$. A general equation of state needs a root finding for the density behind a shock on $[e] = -\tfrac{1}{2}(p^* + p_\mathrm{K})[1/\rho]$, with $[\,\cdot\,]$ the jump across the shock ([Colella and Glaz](#colella-glaz-1985) 1985). The exact solver runs as follows.

1. Write the velocity behind each outer wave as a function of $p^*$, on the shock branch above the outer pressure and on the rarefaction branch below it.
2. Solve $v^*_{x,\mathrm{L}}(p^*) - v^*_{x,\mathrm{R}}(p^*) = 0$ for $p^*$ with a root finder such as Newton–Raphson, which needs a good guess and a derivative that does not vanish.
3. Find $v_x^*$, $\rho^*_\mathrm{L}$ and $\rho^*_\mathrm{R}$ from the two branches.
4. Sample the solution on the face, $x/t = 0$: the sign of $v_x^*$ picks the side of the contact, and the outer wave on that side decides between outer and star state, or puts the face inside a rarefaction fan.
5. Form the flux from the state on the face.

A contact at rest is best tested for explicitly and given the mean of the two star states, which keeps the solution symmetric. **[GK]** Inside a left fan of a gamma-law gas, $v_x = [2/(\gamma + 1)][c_{\mathrm{s,L}} + \tfrac{1}{2}(\gamma - 1)v_{x,\mathrm{L}}]$ on the face, with $\rho$ and $p$ following from $c_\mathrm{s} = v_x$ and the constant entropy.

Detail: [Zingale 2.1.5](../references/computational-hydrodynamics-synthesis.md#215-root-finding), [Zingale 2.4.1](../references/computational-hydrodynamics-synthesis.md#241-characteristics-shocks-and-rarefactions), [Zingale 2.4.2](../references/computational-hydrodynamics-synthesis.md#242-shock-speed-and-the-riemann-problem), [Zingale 2.5.4](../references/computational-hydrodynamics-synthesis.md#254-the-riemann-problem), [Zingale 2.5.5](../references/computational-hydrodynamics-synthesis.md#255-rarefactions), [Zingale 2.5.6](../references/computational-hydrodynamics-synthesis.md#256-shocks), [Zingale 2.5.7](../references/computational-hydrodynamics-synthesis.md#257-the-star-state), [Zingale 2.5.8](../references/computational-hydrodynamics-synthesis.md#258-sampling-the-solution).

### 2.2 Approximate star states

The wave curves of two-shock solvers, such as that of Colella and Glaz (1985), lie close to the exact ones for Sod's problem except at small pressures, where the exact branch is a rarefaction, and most approximate solvers interpolate linearly across a fan that spans the face instead of resolving it (Zingale's text, §8.3). pyro's solver after Colella, Glaz and Ferguson takes the acoustic star state, with the impedances $\rho_\mathrm{K}c_{\mathrm{s,K}}$ written $Z_\mathrm{K}$:

$$p^* = \frac{Z_\mathrm{L}p_\mathrm{R} + Z_\mathrm{R}p_\mathrm{L} + Z_\mathrm{L}Z_\mathrm{R}(v_{x,\mathrm{L}} - v_{x,\mathrm{R}})}{Z_\mathrm{L} + Z_\mathrm{R}}, \qquad v_x^* = \frac{Z_\mathrm{L}v_{x,\mathrm{L}} + Z_\mathrm{R}v_{x,\mathrm{R}} + p_\mathrm{L} - p_\mathrm{R}}{Z_\mathrm{L} + Z_\mathrm{R}}.$$

Density and internal energy change linearly across each acoustic wave, and the solution is sampled as in 2.1, with a fan across the face blended linearly. The estimate in primitive variables of [Toro](#toro-1999) (1999), $p^* = \tfrac{1}{2}(p_\mathrm{L} + p_\mathrm{R}) - \tfrac{1}{2}(v_{x,\mathrm{R}} - v_{x,\mathrm{L}})\,\bar\rho\,\bar c_\mathrm{s}$ with the arithmetic means $\bar\rho$ and $\bar c_\mathrm{s}$, together with $v_x^* = \tfrac{1}{2}(v_{x,\mathrm{L}} + v_{x,\mathrm{R}}) - \tfrac{1}{2}(p_\mathrm{R} - p_\mathrm{L})/(\bar\rho\,\bar c_\mathrm{s})$ and the densities $\rho^*_\mathrm{L} = \rho_\mathrm{L} + (v_{x,\mathrm{L}} - v_x^*)\bar\rho/\bar c_\mathrm{s}$ and $\rho^*_\mathrm{R} = \rho_\mathrm{R} + (v_x^* - v_{x,\mathrm{R}})\bar\rho/\bar c_\mathrm{s}$, serves Athena++ to estimate the wave speeds of 2.4. pyro starts from it as well, but replaces it by a two-rarefaction estimate where $p^*$ lies below both pressures, and by a two-shock estimate where it lies above both and the larger pressure exceeds twice the smaller.

Detail: [Zingale 2.5.14](../references/computational-hydrodynamics-synthesis.md#2514-riemann-solvers-and-the-conservative-update), [pyro 2.4.7](../references/pyro-synthesis.md#247-riemann-solvers), [Athena 2.5.3](../references/athena-synthesis.md#253-hllc-and-hlld).

### 2.3 HLL, HLLE and LLF

The HLL solver of [Harten, Lax and van Leer](#harten-et-al-1983) (1983) replaces the fan by a single state between the fastest waves, whose speeds $S_\mathrm{L}$ and $S_\mathrm{R}$ are estimated, and the jump conditions across the two waves fix that state ([Diemer](#diemer-2025) 2025, eq. 11):

$$\mathbf{F}^\mathrm{HLL} = \begin{cases} \mathbf{F}_\mathrm{L}, & S_\mathrm{L} \ge 0, \\[2pt] \dfrac{S_\mathrm{R}\mathbf{F}_\mathrm{L} - S_\mathrm{L}\mathbf{F}_\mathrm{R} + S_\mathrm{L}S_\mathrm{R}(\mathbf{U}_\mathrm{R} - \mathbf{U}_\mathrm{L})}{S_\mathrm{R} - S_\mathrm{L}}, & S_\mathrm{L} < 0 < S_\mathrm{R}, \\[2pt] \mathbf{F}_\mathrm{R}, & S_\mathrm{R} \le 0, \end{cases}$$

with $\mathbf{F}_\mathrm{K} = \mathbf{F}(\mathbf{U}_\mathrm{K})$. The codes differ in their estimates of the speeds. Ulula takes $S_\mathrm{L} = v_{x,\mathrm{L}} - c_{\mathrm{s,L}}$ and $S_\mathrm{R} = v_{x,\mathrm{R}} + c_{\mathrm{s,R}}$. The HLLE form of [Einfeldt et al.](#einfeldt-et-al-1991) (1991) in Athena and Athena++ bounds them by the extreme eigenvalues of the Roe average of 2.6 as well, $S_\mathrm{L} = \min(\lambda^{(1)}, v_{x,\mathrm{L}} - c_{\mathrm{max,L}}, 0)$ and $S_\mathrm{R} = \max(\lambda^{(M)}, v_{x,\mathrm{R}} + c_{\mathrm{max,R}}, 0)$, with $c_{\mathrm{max,K}}$ the sound speed in hydrodynamics and the fast speed in MHD, and $M$ the number of waves, so that a single formula covers all three cases. The single state diffuses the contact, even at rest, and in MHD the slow and Alfvén waves too, but it keeps density and pressure positive, so that HLLE serves as a fallback where accurate solvers fail ([Stone et al.](#stone-et-al-2008) 2008, §4.3.1). The local Lax–Friedrichs flux of Athena++, $\tfrac{1}{2}(\mathbf{F}_\mathrm{L} + \mathbf{F}_\mathrm{R}) - \tfrac{1}{2}S_\mathrm{max}(\mathbf{U}_\mathrm{R} - \mathbf{U}_\mathrm{L})$ with $S_\mathrm{max} = \max(|v_{x,\mathrm{L}}| + c_{\mathrm{max,L}}, |v_{x,\mathrm{R}}| + c_{\mathrm{max,R}})$, diffuses even more and serves for tests. **[GK]** Ulula's estimates cross where two states collide faster than the sum of their sound speeds, and its flux then carries mass through a symmetric collision, which the exact solution does not.

Detail: [Zingale 2.5.14](../references/computational-hydrodynamics-synthesis.md#2514-riemann-solvers-and-the-conservative-update), [Ulula 2.4.1](../references/ulula-synthesis.md#241-the-hll-solver), [Athena 2.5.1](../references/athena-synthesis.md#251-approximate-solvers), [Athena 2.5.2](../references/athena-synthesis.md#252-hlle-and-llf).

### 2.4 HLLC

HLLC restores the contact between the outer waves, so that two star states lie on either side of a contact moving at $S_\mathrm{M}$ ([Toro, Spruce and Speares](#toro-et-al-1994) 1994; Diemer 2025, eqs. 12 to 15). With $\mathrm{K}$ for L or R,

$$\mathbf{F}^\mathrm{HLLC} = \mathbf{F}_\mathrm{K} + S_\mathrm{K}(\mathbf{U}^*_\mathrm{K} - \mathbf{U}_\mathrm{K}), \qquad \mathbf{U}^*_\mathrm{K} = \rho_\mathrm{K}\frac{S_\mathrm{K} - v_{x,\mathrm{K}}}{S_\mathrm{K} - S_\mathrm{M}}\begin{pmatrix} 1 \\ S_\mathrm{M} \\ \mathbf{v}_{\perp,\mathrm{K}} \\ E_\mathrm{K}/\rho_\mathrm{K} + (S_\mathrm{M} - v_{x,\mathrm{K}})\left[S_\mathrm{M} + p_\mathrm{K}/\big(\rho_\mathrm{K}(S_\mathrm{K} - v_{x,\mathrm{K}})\big)\right] \end{pmatrix},$$

on the side of the contact that holds the face, and $\mathbf{F}_\mathrm{L}$ or $\mathbf{F}_\mathrm{R}$ outside the outer waves, with $\mathbf{v}_\perp$ the transverse velocity. The contact speed follows from the condition that both star states share the pressure ([Batten et al.](#batten-et-al-1997) 1997):

$$S_\mathrm{M} = \frac{p_\mathrm{R} - p_\mathrm{L} + \rho_\mathrm{L}v_{x,\mathrm{L}}(S_\mathrm{L} - v_{x,\mathrm{L}}) - \rho_\mathrm{R}v_{x,\mathrm{R}}(S_\mathrm{R} - v_{x,\mathrm{R}})}{\rho_\mathrm{L}(S_\mathrm{L} - v_{x,\mathrm{L}}) - \rho_\mathrm{R}(S_\mathrm{R} - v_{x,\mathrm{R}})}.$$

The codes differ in the outer speeds. Ulula takes those of its HLL, while pyro and Athena++ start from the star pressure of 2.2 and widen the speed of a shock by $q_\mathrm{K} = [1 + (\gamma + 1)(p^*/p_\mathrm{K} - 1)/(2\gamma)]^{1/2}$ where $p^* > p_\mathrm{K}$, as $S_\mathrm{L} = v_{x,\mathrm{L}} - c_{\mathrm{s,L}}q_\mathrm{L}$ and $S_\mathrm{R} = v_{x,\mathrm{R}} + c_{\mathrm{s,R}}q_\mathrm{R}$. With a general equation of state, Athena++ replaces $\gamma$ in $q_\mathrm{K}$ by $\Gamma^*_\mathrm{K} = \rho^*_\mathrm{K}c_\mathrm{s}^2(\rho^*_\mathrm{K}, p^*)/p^*$, with the densities of the estimate of 2.2, which needs no further function of the equation of state ([Coleman](#coleman-2020) 2020). Athena recommends HLLC for adiabatic hydrodynamics, as it is as accurate as Roe's solver at much lower cost and positive in one dimension. Ulula allows HLLC only for an ideal gas, as its formulae need the pressure and the energy and, by Diemer (2025), an isothermal gas has no contact. **[GK]** An isothermal gas has no contact in one dimension, but in each sweep of a two-dimensional grid its transverse velocity, and any passive scalar, jumps across a wave at the flow speed, which an HLLC built for the isothermal gas keeps sharp. pyro's code divides $\gamma + 1$ by $2/\gamma$ instead of $2\gamma$ in $q_\mathrm{R}$, which widens $S_\mathrm{R}$ behind a shock and adds dissipation.

Detail: [pyro 2.4.7](../references/pyro-synthesis.md#247-riemann-solvers), [Ulula 2.4.2](../references/ulula-synthesis.md#242-the-hllc-solver), [Athena 2.5.3](../references/athena-synthesis.md#253-hllc-and-hlld).

### 2.5 HLLD

[Miyoshi and Kusano](#miyoshi-kusano-2005) (2005) extend HLL to MHD with five waves: the fast waves $S_\mathrm{L}$ and $S_\mathrm{R}$, from the fast speeds of both states, the Alfvén waves $S^*_\mathrm{L}$ and $S^*_\mathrm{R}$, and the contact $S_\mathrm{M}$, which bound four intermediate states, $\mathbf{U}^*_\mathrm{L}$, $\mathbf{U}^{**}_\mathrm{L}$, $\mathbf{U}^{**}_\mathrm{R}$ and $\mathbf{U}^*_\mathrm{R}$. The normal velocity and the total pressure $p_\mathrm{T}$ are constant across the intermediate states, so that $S_\mathrm{M}$ is the HLLC speed with $p_\mathrm{T}$ in place of $p$, and

$$\rho^*_\mathrm{K} = \rho_\mathrm{K}\frac{S_\mathrm{K} - v_{x,\mathrm{K}}}{S_\mathrm{K} - S_\mathrm{M}}, \qquad S^*_\mathrm{L} = S_\mathrm{M} - \frac{|B_x|}{\sqrt{\rho^*_\mathrm{L}}}, \qquad S^*_\mathrm{R} = S_\mathrm{M} + \frac{|B_x|}{\sqrt{\rho^*_\mathrm{R}}}.$$

The jump conditions across each wave give the transverse velocity and field of the states, and the flux is that of the state that holds the face, such as $\mathbf{F}_\mathrm{L} + S_\mathrm{L}(\mathbf{U}^*_\mathrm{L} - \mathbf{U}_\mathrm{L})$. Where $\rho_\mathrm{K}(S_\mathrm{K} - v_{x,\mathrm{K}})(S_\mathrm{K} - S_\mathrm{M}) - B_x^2$ nearly vanishes, Athena++ keeps the transverse components of the outer state. HLLD reduces exactly to HLLC for $B_x = 0$, stays positive and in Athena typically matches the accuracy of Roe's solver for MHD at much lower cost. Its isothermal version, after [Mignone](#mignone-2007) (2007), has three intermediate states.

Detail: [Athena 2.5.3](../references/athena-synthesis.md#253-hllc-and-hlld).

### 2.6 Roe's linearised solver

The solver of [Roe](#roe-1981) (1981) solves exactly the linearised problem $\partial\mathbf{U}/\partial t + \mathsf{A}(\bar{\mathbf{U}})\,\partial\mathbf{U}/\partial x = 0$, whose solution holds only discontinuities, one for each eigenvalue of the Jacobian at a mean state (Stone et al. 2008, §4.3.2):

$$\mathbf{F}^\mathrm{Roe} = \tfrac{1}{2}(\mathbf{F}_\mathrm{L} + \mathbf{F}_\mathrm{R}) - \tfrac{1}{2}\sum_m |\lambda^{(m)}|\,\big[\mathbf{l}^{(m)}\cdot(\mathbf{U}_\mathrm{R} - \mathbf{U}_\mathrm{L})\big]\,\mathbf{r}^{(m)},$$

with the eigensystem of the Jacobian at the Roe average (Stone et al. 2008, App. B): $\tilde\rho = (\rho_\mathrm{L}\rho_\mathrm{R})^{1/2}$, and the velocity and the enthalpy $(E + p)/\rho$ weighted by $\rho^{1/2}$. This mean makes the flux exact where the solution is a single discontinuity, and [Cargo and Gallice](#cargo-gallice-1997) (1997) extend it to MHD. All waves enter, so that the solver diffuses contacts and the other intermediate waves less than HLLE. Being linear, it can produce negative densities or pressures in its intermediate states. Athena then computes the flux of that face with HLLE or an exact solver, which tests show to be needed very rarely, while Athena++ falls back on LLF and checks the density alone.

Detail: [Athena 2.5.4](../references/athena-synthesis.md#254-roes-method).

### 2.7 The carbuncle and solvers of low dissipation

Strong shocks aligned with the grid can make Godunov methods unstable in several dimensions, as perturbations of the front grow to large amplitudes at the scale of the grid ([Quirk](#quirk-1994) 1994). The one-dimensional solvers add too little dissipation to the fluxes along the front. Athena detects such shocks and raises every $|\lambda^{(m)}|$ of Roe's flux to at least $\eta^\mathrm{H}$, the largest of $\eta = \tfrac{1}{2}|(v_{x,i} + c_{\mathrm{fast},i}) - (v_{x,i-1} - c_{\mathrm{fast},i-1})|$ over a stencil of five faces shaped like the letter H, nine in three dimensions, after [Sanders, Morano and Druguet](#sanders-et-al-1998) (1998). HLL fluxes take the correction through their speeds. Athena++ has no H-correction but offers HLLC and HLLD of low dissipation after [Minoshima and Miyoshi](#minoshima-miyoshi-2021) (2021). Two factors modify their contact speed and middle pressure:

$$\psi_\mathrm{s} = \min\left\{1,\ \frac{c_\mathrm{max} - \min(\delta v_\mathrm{n}, 0)}{c_\mathrm{max} - \min(\delta v_\mathrm{t}, 0)}\right\}^4, \qquad \psi_\mathrm{M} = \mathrm{Ma}\,(2 - \mathrm{Ma}), \quad \mathrm{Ma} = \min\left[1, \frac{\max(|\mathbf{v}_\mathrm{L}|, |\mathbf{v}_\mathrm{R}|)}{c_\mathrm{max}}\right],$$

with $\delta v_\mathrm{n}$ the jump of the normal velocity across the face and $\delta v_\mathrm{t}$ the most compressive one of the transverse velocities beside it, and $c_\mathrm{max}$ the larger sound or fast speed of the two states. $\psi_\mathrm{s}$ weakens the pressure jump in the contact speed where the flow is compressed more along the face than across it, which suppresses the carbuncle, and $\psi_\mathrm{M}$ lowers the dissipation at low Mach numbers. LHLLD applies the two factors to $S_\mathrm{M}$ and to the total pressure of the intermediate states, and forms Ma from a speed that includes the magnetic and kinetic energies. pyro's HLLC of low Mach number applies $\psi_\mathrm{M}$ alone.

Detail: [pyro 2.4.7](../references/pyro-synthesis.md#247-riemann-solvers), [Athena 2.5.5](../references/athena-synthesis.md#255-the-carbuncle).

## 3. Tests

Sod's shock tube, a Riemann problem whose evolution is the exact solution of 2.1, checks an exact solver. **[GK]** For $\gamma = 1.4$ the root is $p^* = 0.30313$, with $v_x^* = 0.92745$, $\rho^*_\mathrm{L} = 0.42632$, $\rho^*_\mathrm{R} = 0.26557$ and a shock speed of $1.75216$, which an implementation reproduces to all digits ([Marzouk](#marzouk-2020) 2020).

The carbuncle test perturbs a strong shock aligned with the grid. Athena reflects a uniform flow of high Mach number from a wall with cell-to-cell perturbations of $10^{-4}$ in the density, so that the reflected shock develops the instability as it crosses the grid. Athena++ runs the variant of [Hanawa, Mikami and Matsumoto](#hanawa-et-al-2008) (2008), a shock that differs from row to row in its cell, and compares the entropy of an odd and an even row there after $t = 0.4$. A difference above 0.05 marks the carbuncle, and its regression tests run every solver.

Detail: [Zingale 2.5.7](../references/computational-hydrodynamics-synthesis.md#257-the-star-state), [Zingale 2.5.19](../references/computational-hydrodynamics-synthesis.md#2519-test-problems), [Athena 2.5.5](../references/athena-synthesis.md#255-the-carbuncle).

## 4. Assessment

Stone et al. (2008) find HLLC as accurate as Roe's solver for hydrodynamics and HLLD as accurate for MHD, both at much lower cost, and report that the fallback from Roe's solver is needed very rarely and the H-correction removes the carbuncle in Noh's problem. The regression tests of Athena++ expect the carbuncle from Roe's solver and plain HLLC and HLLD, and none from HLLE, LLF and the solvers of low dissipation. [Stone et al.](#stone-et-al-2020) (2020) find that exact solvers improve the accuracy little, although they may make strong rarefactions more robust. With a general equation of state, HLLE fails to converge in Coleman's double shocks, and the speeds from the estimate of 2.2 reduce the errors of HLLC there (Coleman 2020). Ulula's HLLC does markedly better than HLL where contacts matter (Diemer 2025). **[GK]** The solvers trade accuracy at intermediate waves against robustness. HLLE and LLF keep their intermediate states positive, which makes a first-order scheme positive in one dimension, but smear contacts and shear, which matters for mixing; HLLC and HLLD resolve them at a cost close to HLL's; Roe's solver resolves all waves but needs a fallback and, in transonic rarefactions, commonly an entropy fix, which the syntheses describe only for Roe solvers of the shallow-water equations. The solvers that resolve the contact are those prone to the carbuncle, so that accuracy and robustness meet in a detector of shocks. Open are which fallback a code keeps, whether Roe's solver is worth its eigensystem beside HLLC and HLLD, and whether the corrections for low Mach numbers suit the slow flows around clouds.
