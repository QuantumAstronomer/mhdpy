# Instabilities, vortices and convection

## 1. Overview

Instabilities and vortices test how a scheme treats slow, smooth motions that shocks do not dominate: shear layers that roll up, heavy fluid above light fluid under gravity, a vortex in equilibrium, and buoyant structures in stratified atmospheres. Without explicit viscosity, most of these problems have no unique solution, as the numerical diffusion and the noise of the grid set the small scales, so that they test the dissipation and the symmetry of a scheme qualitatively; a problem with explicit diffusion and a converged reference solution makes the comparison quantitative. pyro and Ulula run the Kelvin–Helmholtz and Rayleigh–Taylor instabilities and the Gresho vortex, pyro also bubbles, convection and plumes, and Athena and Athena++ the Rayleigh–Taylor instability and a benchmark of the Kelvin–Helmholtz instability with explicit diffusion. Gravity as a source follows in topic 12 and hydrostatic boundaries in topic 4.

## 2. Problems

### 2.1 The Kelvin–Helmholtz instability

Two shear layers in a periodic box, between a dense band moving one way and lighter gas moving the other, roll up into vortices from a small perturbation of the transverse velocity. Ulula sets smooth layers after [Robertson et al.](#robertson-et-al-2010) (2010) with a single seeded mode, or sharp ones that the noise of the grid seeds, as in [Springel](#springel-2010) (2010), on $200 \times 200$ cells, and its docstring recommends the smooth version as the more physical test. pyro follows [McNally et al.](#mcnally-et-al-2012) (2012), whose optional bulk velocity tests the numerical dissipation. Athena++ repeats the benchmark of [Lecoanet et al.](#lecoanet-et-al-2015) (2015), smooth layers with explicit viscosity, conduction and diffusion of a dye at a Reynolds number of $10^5$, against reference solutions of the pseudo-spectral code Dedalus, with VL2 and PLM and with RK3 and PPM at several resolutions ([Stone et al.](#stone-et-al-2020) 2020, §3.3.6).

Detail: [Ulula 2.8.9](../references/ulula-synthesis.md#289-the-kelvinhelmholtz-instability), [pyro 2.4.12](../references/pyro-synthesis.md#2412-problems-and-tests), [Athena 2.16.5](../references/athena-synthesis.md#2165-hydrodynamics-in-two-and-three-dimensions).

### 2.2 The Rayleigh–Taylor instability

Fluid of density 2 above fluid of density 1 in hydrostatic equilibrium under constant gravity, with a perturbation of the interface, grows into rising bubbles and falling spikes that roll up at their sides. Athena runs the single mode of [Liska and Wendroff](#liska-wendroff-2003) (2003, §4.6) on $200 \times 400$ cells with source terms that conserve the total energy exactly ([Stone et al.](#stone-et-al-2008) 2008, §8.3). Ulula runs a single mode on $80 \times 240$ cells and, in its paper, two superposed modes of about 1.5 and 12 wavelengths across the box, whose initial growth linear theory predicts ([Diemer](#diemer-2025) 2025, §4.1). pyro runs a single mode, two modes side by side, many modes with random phases, and the stable stratification that carries gravity waves, with hydrostatic boundaries.

Detail: [Ulula 2.8.13](../references/ulula-synthesis.md#2813-the-rayleightaylor-instability), [pyro 2.4.12](../references/pyro-synthesis.md#2412-problems-and-tests), [Athena 2.16.5](../references/athena-synthesis.md#2165-hydrodynamics-in-two-and-three-dimensions).

### 2.3 The Gresho vortex

The vortex of [Gresho and Sani](#gresho-sani-1987) (1987) rotates at a speed rising linearly to 1 at $r = 0.2$ and falling back to zero at 0.4, with a pressure that balances the rotation, so that it is steady and its known solution is the initial state. Ulula's docstring calls it a test of symmetry and of the conservation of angular momentum, which any smoothing of its ring reveals as imperfect. Ulula runs it on $200 \times 200$ cells to $t = 6$, and pyro after [Miczek et al.](#miczek-et-al-2015) (2015) at a peak Mach number of 0.1 on $40 \times 40$ cells.

Detail: [Ulula 2.8.12](../references/ulula-synthesis.md#2812-the-gresho-vortex), [pyro 2.4.12](../references/pyro-synthesis.md#2412-problems-and-tests).

### 2.4 Bubbles, convection and plumes

pyro sets stratified atmospheres in discrete hydrostatic equilibrium and lets heat drive the flow. A hot, light bubble rises through an isothermal atmosphere, a heated layer drives convection in an adiabatic atmosphere with random initial velocities, a sponge and a floor of the density, and a heated spot drives a plume. The problems test the balance of gravity and pressure, the hydrostatic boundaries and the sponge of topic 12 over long times, without known solutions.

Detail: [pyro 2.4.12](../references/pyro-synthesis.md#2412-problems-and-tests).

## 3. Assessment

In the benchmark of Lecoanet et al., RK3 with PPM in Athena++ converges to the reference on $8192 \times 16384$ cells, half the resolution that CTU with split diffusion needed in Athena, partly thanks to the unsplit diffusion, while VL2 with PLM on half that grid develops the instability of the inner vortex at $t = 4$, though far more weakly than CTU did; the run on 8192 cells across took half the time of Dedalus with 4096 modes, so that finite volumes reach spectral accuracy at lower cost (Stone et al. 2020). Athena's Rayleigh–Taylor instability shows more fine structure than many codes of Liska and Wendroff but less than PPM with Lagrangian steps and remapping, which may reflect a larger diffusion of contacts or the steepeners of those codes (Stone et al. 2008). In Ulula's two modes the short one grows first, before the long one and the walls form a complex pattern. Ulula's Gresho vortex keeps its density within 0.002 and its radial velocity within about 0.02, while the peak of the azimuthal velocity is smoothed from 1 to about 0.92, a result its paper calls good for a test that grid codes often struggle with. pyro explains the shock at the top of its rising bubble by material above the floor of the density that rains down. **[GK]** pyro's Gresho vortex is not in equilibrium, as its pressure supplies only about 0.63 of the centripetal acceleration, so that it tests no steady state. The problems without explicit dissipation separate schemes only qualitatively, by the fine structure and the symmetry that they keep, whereas the benchmark of Lecoanet et al. separates them quantitatively and shows that explicit diffusion turns these problems into tests of convergence. The Gresho vortex measures dissipation at low Mach numbers, where Godunov schemes are known to diffuse. For the production runs, the Kelvin–Helmholtz and Rayleigh–Taylor instabilities at the surface of a cloud set its mixing with the corona, so that a converged benchmark with explicit conduction, after Lecoanet et al., suits mhdpy better than the unresolved versions.
