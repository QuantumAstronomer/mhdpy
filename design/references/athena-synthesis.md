# Synthesis of Athena and Athena++

This synthesis digests Athena++, a code for astrophysical magnetohydrodynamics (MHD) on meshes with adaptive refinement, written in C++, and Athena, its predecessor written in C. The code of Athena++ is kept as `reference-works/Athena/Code`, in a copy that is cited at release v24.0 (1.1). Thirteen papers complete the sources, kept in `reference-works/Athena/Docs`: seven on Athena, whose code is not among the sources, and six on Athena++ (1.3). Additions found by web search, which fill gaps in these sources or check their claims, are marked **[GK]** and cited. A **[GK]** mark covers the rest of its paragraph or list item. Code is cited by its path in the repository and the name of a function or class, as in (`src/hydro/rsolvers/mhd/hlld.cpp`, `Hydro::RiemannSolver`). A paper is cited by author and year, with the numbers of its sections, equations, figures and tables in the local version, as in [Stone et al.](#stone-et-al-2008) (2008, [arXiv:0804.0402](https://arxiv.org/abs/0804.0402), §4.2.3, eq. 46), and its arXiv identifier is given at its first citation. Within a paragraph, later citations of the same paper give the numbers alone. Each method is described once, from the papers and the code together: the papers on Athena tell what Athena does, and the code and the papers on Athena++ what Athena++ does. Where the two codes differ, the text says so.

## 1. Overview

### 1.1 Origin, versions and licence

### 1.2 Aims and scope

[Stone et al.](#stone-et-al-2008) (2008, §1) explain the need for Athena from the limits of ZEUS ([Stone and Norman](#stone-norman-1992a) 1992a, [b](#stone-norman-1992b); [Clarke](#clarke-1996) 1996; [Hayes et al.](#hayes-et-al-2006) 2006), digested in `zeus-synthesis.md`. The operator-split algorithm of ZEUS, with upwind advection of higher order, centred differences for the other terms and artificial viscosity at shocks, had served in many hundreds of astrophysical studies and was easy to extend with further physics. In the fifteen years since, refining the mesh, statically (SMR) or adaptively (AMR), had become the means to resolve wide ranges of scale on grids. Conservation at the internal boundaries between coarse and fine meshes is needed to prevent spurious reflections there ([Berger and Colella](#berger-colella-1989) 1989), so a scheme that does not solve the equations in conservative form, such as that of ZEUS, does not suit refined meshes. This was the main motive for Athena. Its algorithms are unsplit Godunov methods of higher order, which suit SMR and AMR and capture shocks, contact discontinuities and rotational discontinuities better. Other MHD codes for AMR on this basis already existed: RIEMANN ([Balsara](#balsara-2000) 2000), BATS-R-US ([Powell et al.](#powell-et-al-1999) 1999; [Gombosi et al.](#gombosi-et-al-2004) 2004), AMRVAC ([Tóth](#toth-1996) 1996; [Nool and Keppens](#nool-keppens-2002) 2002), Nirvana ([Ziegler](#ziegler-2005) 2005), RAMSES ([Fromang, Hennebelle and Teyssier](#fromang-et-al-2006) 2006), PLUTO ([Mignone et al.](#mignone-et-al-2007) 2007) and AstroBEAR ([Cunningham et al.](#cunningham-et-al-2007) 2007). The authors justify another code by their experience that the details of an algorithm matter, and those of Athena differ from the others in small or substantial ways. They wanted the code to be accurate, simple to use and to adapt, and easy to maintain, and meant the paper as a reference for anyone who adopts, modifies or extends it.

Godunov methods for MHD had advanced mainly in two respects, the integrator in several dimensions and the way the magnetic field is kept free of divergence (§1). The options tried ranged from integrators split by direction that do not constrain the divergence at all to split and unsplit integrators combined with a Hodge projection of the field, with a non-conservative form of the equations that lets errors in the constraint propagate and decay, or with constrained transport (CT) after [Evans and Hawley](#evans-hawley-1988) (1988). [Tóth](#toth-2000) (2000) compared many of them on a broad suite of tests. Athena discretises mass, momentum and energy as volume averages over cells and the magnetic field as area averages over the faces of cells, which CT evolves (abstract). Its paper names three new elements. The first is a consistent way to obtain the electric fields that CT needs, averaged over time and over the edges of cells, from the Godunov fluxes, which are averages over time and faces. The second extends to MHD the reconstruction schemes whose prediction in time is split by direction, such as PPM after [Colella and Woodward](#colella-woodward-1984) (1984). The third extends two unsplit integrators to MHD: the corner transport upwind (CTU) method of [Colella](#colella-1990) (1990), which Athena combines with CT as CTU+CT, and a simpler predictor-corrector scheme from the appendix of [Falle](#falle-1991) (1991), close to the MUSCL–Hancock scheme ([van Leer](#van-leer-2006) 2006; [Toro](#toro-1999) 1999), as VL+CT (§1). [Gardiner and Stone](#gardiner-stone-2005) (2005, [arXiv:astro-ph/0501557](https://arxiv.org/abs/astro-ph/0501557)) set out the mathematics in two dimensions and [Gardiner and Stone](#gardiner-stone-2008) (2008, [arXiv:0712.2634](https://arxiv.org/abs/0712.2634)) in three, while the paper on the code describes the implementation. CTU+CT proved less diffusive and more accurate, so that paper describes it and leaves VL+CT, which has advantages in some applications, to a companion paper, which it cites as submitted in 2008. **[GK]** That paper appeared as [Stone and Gardiner](#stone-gardiner-2009) (2009), which is not among the sources, as 1.3 explains.

The summary of the paper on Athena stresses what the combination achieves (§9). With volume and area averages, mass, momentum, energy and magnetic flux stay exactly conserved on the grid, which AMR requires. The tests show that smooth flows of every family of MHD waves converge at second order in space and time, also in several dimensions. The unsplit methods keep the symmetries of a flow: the CTU+CT method in two dimensions reduces exactly to the method in one dimension for plane-parallel flows aligned with the grid, and in three dimensions to those in two or one. On this property rests a sensitive test of the stencil that keeps the field free of divergence. A planar loop of field advected by a velocity field in three dimensions must stay planar, since any field perpendicular to its plane grows in proportion to $\nabla \cdot \mathbf{B}$. The refinement of the mesh, the main motive for the code, was left to a later paper, together with the CTU+CT integrator on refined meshes. Gravity from a fixed potential and self-gravity, the shearing box ([Gardiner and Stone](#gardiner-stone-2006) 2006), anisotropic thermal conduction ([Parrish and Stone](#parrish-stone-2005) 2005, [2007](#parrish-stone-2007)) and the transfer of ionising radiation ([Krumholz, Stone and Gardiner](#krumholz-et-al-2007) 2007) had been added, and curvilinear coordinates, relativistic MHD and radiation MHD with full transport were under way. Athena already served to study the magnetorotational instability (MRI) in the shearing box, colliding winds in close binaries, the decay of turbulence, the magnetic Rayleigh–Taylor instability, shocks striking magnetised clouds and supersonic turbulence in molecular clouds. The code was available on the web, with documentation and further test problems.

[Stone et al.](#stone-et-al-2020) (2020, §1) place Athena++ in a trend towards ever greater complexity among the codes of astrophysical fluid dynamics. Realistic models need further physics, among it the transfer of radiation, self-gravity, networks of chemical or nuclear reactions and dynamical spacetimes, grids adopt one of several strategies of AMR, codes offer many options of coordinates, Riemann solvers and orders of accuracy, and computers grow more heterogeneous. Among the widely used grid codes they name ZEUS ([Stone and Norman](#stone-norman-1992a) 1992a, [b](#stone-norman-1992b); [Stone, Mihalas and Norman](#stone-et-al-1992) 1992; [Hayes et al.](#hayes-et-al-2006) 2006), ART ([Kravtsov, Klypin and Khokhlov](#kravtsov-et-al-1997) 1997), FLASH ([Fryxell et al.](#fryxell-et-al-2000) 2000), RAMSES ([Teyssier](#teyssier-2002) 2002), HARM ([Gammie, McKinney and Tóth](#gammie-et-al-2003) 2003), PLUTO ([Mignone et al.](#mignone-et-al-2007) 2007, [2012](#mignone-et-al-2012)), Athena and Enzo ([Bryan et al.](#bryan-et-al-2014) 2014). Athena itself had grown in this way. It gained further time integrators ([Stone and Gardiner](#stone-gardiner-2009) 2009), the shearing box ([Stone and Gardiner](#stone-gardiner-2010) 2010, [arXiv:1006.0139](https://arxiv.org/abs/1006.0139)), cylindrical coordinates ([Skinner and Ostriker](#skinner-ostriker-2010) 2010, [arXiv:1004.2487](https://arxiv.org/abs/1004.2487)), special relativity ([Beckwith and Stone](#beckwith-stone-2011) 2011, [arXiv:1101.3573](https://arxiv.org/abs/1101.3573)), particles ([Bai and Stone](#bai-stone-2010) 2010, [arXiv:1005.4980](https://arxiv.org/abs/1005.4980)) and sink particles ([Gong and Ostriker](#gong-ostriker-2013) 2013). It also gained a formulation of self-gravity that conserves the total energy ([Jiang et al.](#jiang-et-al-2013) 2013) and the transport of radiation ([Skinner and Ostriker](#skinner-ostriker-2013) 2013; [Davis, Stone and Jiang](#davis-et-al-2012) 2012; [Jiang, Stone and Davis](#jiang-et-al-2012) 2012, [2014a](#jiang-et-al-2014a)), among other features. Maintaining it had become untenable, and its AMR, built on overlapping patches ([Berger and Oliger](#berger-oliger-1984) 1984; [Berger and Colella](#berger-colella-1989) 1989), performed poorly on highly parallel machines. Athena++ is therefore a complete redesign. Its framework separates the mesh from the physics solved on it, refines the mesh by blocks rather than patches, and schedules the steps of an algorithm dynamically through lists of tasks (3). The language C++ was chosen for the modularity and organisation that its extensions allow.

The paper on Athena++ introduces its framework for AMR, with refinement by blocks of variables at cell centres and on faces, as MHD requires, its use in various coordinate systems and its dynamic execution by task lists (§1). As examples of modules of physics, it describes solvers for MHD, both non-relativistic and relativistic, based on the methods of Athena. It confines itself to what is new in the framework, since [Gardiner and Stone](#gardiner-stone-2005) (2005, [2008](#gardiner-stone-2008)), [Stone et al.](#stone-et-al-2008) (2008), [Beckwith and Stone](#beckwith-stone-2011) (2011) and [White, Stone and Gammie](#white-et-al-2016) (2016, [arXiv:1511.00943](https://arxiv.org/abs/1511.00943)) describe the algorithms in detail. Its abstract adds that these modules support curvilinear coordinates, time integrators of higher order, a general EOS and diffusion that super time-stepping can integrate, and that they run with well over 80% parallel efficiency on more than half a million threads. Further modules were in preparation (§5). Self-gravity was solved by FFT, following Athena, in the public version, and by full multigrid (FMG), which costs $O(N)$ operations against $O(N \log N)$ for FFT. FMG was being extended to AMR ([Tomida et al.](#tomida-et-al-2023), in preparation), and was to serve implicit moment methods of radiation transfer as well ([Jiang, Stone and Davis](#jiang-et-al-2012) 2012). The time-dependent radiation transport of [Jiang, Stone and Davis](#jiang-et-al-2014a) (2014a) was implemented and used for radiation-dominated accretion discs ([Jiang, Stone and Davis](#jiang-et-al-2014b) 2014b) and massive stars ([Jiang et al.](#jiang-et-al-2018) 2018), and was being extended to general relativity. Flux-limited diffusion and the variable Eddington tensor ([Davis, Stone and Jiang](#davis-et-al-2012) 2012; [Jiang, Stone and Davis](#jiang-et-al-2012) 2012), a Monte Carlo solver for post-processing and the adaptive ray tracing of Athena ([Kim et al.](#kim-et-al-2017) 2017) were to follow. Chemical reaction networks were implemented with a public solver for sparse matrices ([Gong et al.](#gong-et-al-2023), in preparation), and nuclear networks were being built on the same basis ([Halevi et al.](#halevi-et-al), in preparation). Together with passive scalars, they make Athena++ a code for chemo-hydrodynamics. A general module for particles coupled to the gas, based on the methods of [Bai and Stone](#bai-stone-2010) (2010), was under development ([Yang et al.](#yang-et-al), in preparation). In the copy, the multigrid solver works on refined meshes (2.10), radiation transport is present with explicit and implicit integration and with groups of frequencies, and chemistry and cosmic rays have modules of their own (2.15), while particles have none. **[GK]** The works in preparation on multigrid and chemistry appeared as [Tomida and Stone](#tomida-stone-2023) (2023, [arXiv:2302.13903](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.13903)), one of the sources, and as [Gong et al.](#gong-et-al-2023) (2023), listed under [Gong et al.](#gong-et-al-2023) in 4.

The summary of the paper on Athena++ restates the design and adds its results (§6). The framework accepts any logically rectangular coordinates and nonuniform spacing. Different regions of a calculation may carry different task lists, so that, for instance, kinetic MHD in dense, weakly collisional regions could be combined with particle-in-cell methods in diffuse, collisionless ones, and the Poisson equation could be solved on cores other than those for the MHD. The MHD modules extend the methods of Athena by reconstruction of higher order on curvilinear and nonuniform meshes, time integrators of higher order by the method of lines, and diffusion advanced by super time-stepping of the Runge–Kutta–Legendre type, and they work with AMR. Optimised for vector instructions, the MHD module is among the fastest public astrophysical MHD codes known to the authors, in tests of several codes built with the same compilers and optimisations, matched only by DISPATCH ([Nordlund et al.](#nordlund-et-al-2018) 2018). All cores of one Intel Skylake processor together are about five times slower than the same algorithm on an NVIDIA Volta GPU ([Grete, Glines and O'Shea](#grete-et-al-2019) 2019), as the ratio of their peak performance leads one to expect, and weak scaling keeps 84% parallel efficiency with as many as 500,000 threads. A fully fourth-order algorithm for MHD ([Felker and Stone](#felker-stone-2018) 2018, [arXiv:1711.07439](https://arxiv.org/abs/1711.07439)) had been implemented in the framework and was being compared with the existing algorithms on astrophysical applications. The copy offers the fourth order for hydrodynamics only, since its reconstruction refuses it with MHD while the fourth-order CT is not yet merged (`src/reconstruct/reconstruction.cpp`, `Reconstruction::Reconstruction`; 2.7). A version of the whole framework built on the library Kokkos ([Edwards, Trott and Sunderland](#edwards-et-al-2014) 2014) was being prepared for release as open source. Athena++ is public on GitHub under the BSD licence, and new modules join the public version once they are thoroughly tested.

## 3. Software architecture

Nine subjects group the software architecture. `plan.md` lists them, with the steps that write them.

[Stone et al.](#stone-et-al-2020) (2020, §1) explain three decisions that shape the framework of Athena++. First, the mesh is abstract and independent of the physics. The discretisations of the variables, such as volume averages at cell centres, area averages on faces and point values at cell centres or vertices, are built and stored on it, and AMR, boundary conditions and parallelisation by decomposing the domain are implemented for these variables without reference to any physics. New coordinates and new physics therefore work with AMR in any geometry at once, and mesh and physics can be developed apart, as in a version of the framework on Kokkos ([Edwards, Trott and Sunderland](#edwards-et-al-2014) 2014) for heterogeneous machines with GPUs. Second, the mesh is refined by blocks ([Stout et al.](#stout-et-al-1997) 1997) rather than by overlapping patches after [Berger and Oliger](#berger-oliger-1984) (1984), as in Athena, PLUTO and Enzo. With patches, a refined region is covered by several levels, on which quantities computed from the conserved variables, the temperature for instance, may differ, and source terms that depend on them, such as cooling or reaction networks, then drive the levels apart. The overlap between the domains of the processes of the Message Passing Interface (MPI) on different levels also grows complex. In Athena++ each point of the domain belongs to exactly one level, and the levels communicate only through their boundaries, which simplifies the code and improves its performance and scaling. FLASH ([Fryxell et al.](#fryxell-et-al-2000) 2000) also refines by blocks, with the library PARAMESH ([MacNeice et al.](#macneice-et-al-2000) 2000), but Athena++ uses a framework of its own, to support variables on faces, as its MHD requires, to depend on fewer libraries and to gain performance at the cost of generality. Third, the steps of an algorithm, including the sends and receives of MPI, are not executed in a fixed order but assembled into lists of tasks, each of which runs once the tasks it depends on are complete. Computation can then overlap communication, which improves scaling on very many processors, and a calculation chooses its physics by the composition of its task lists. Separate regions can even run separate lists, for example a particle-in-cell code for the collisionless corona above an accretion disc in MHD ([Miller and Stone](#miller-stone-2000) 2000). Libraries such as Legion and Charm++ offer such dynamic execution among much else, but Athena++ implements only the scheduling it needs, in a few thousand lines of its own.
