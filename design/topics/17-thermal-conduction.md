# Thermal conduction

## 1. Overview

Electrons carry heat down the gradient of temperature with a conductivity that rises as $T^{5/2}$, so that conduction can rule the exchange of energy between hot and cold phases, as at the surface of a cloud in a corona. Classically it is the nonlinear diffusion of [topic 16, §2.3](16-diffusion.md#23-variable-and-nonlinear-coefficients), but tangled magnetic fields suppress it, the flux saturates where the mean free path of the electrons approaches the scale of the temperature, and in an ordered field heat flows along the field lines. The collection on conduction and cooling sets out the fluxes of Spitzer (1962) and Cowie and McKee (1977) and the forms that join them, the monotonic anisotropic schemes of Sharma and Hammett (2007), and integration in time by the semi-implicit sweeps of Armillotta et al. (2016) in Athena, explicitly, or by super time-stepping in AthenaPK. PLUTO also conducts along the field, while Athena++ declares anisotropic conduction without implementing it. The splitting of conduction follows in [topic 18, §2.1](18-operator-splitting-and-coupling-of-processes.md#21-strang-splitting) and the clouds that it shapes in [topic 34](34-cold-clouds-and-mixing-layers.md). The references assume one temperature for electrons and ions, and leave out conduction by neutral atoms and implicit anisotropic schemes.

## 2. Methods

### 2.1 The classical flux and its suppression

In a fully ionised plasma whose electrons have a short mean free path, the heat flux follows [Spitzer](#spitzer-1962) (1962), as [Armillotta, Fraternali and Marinacci](#armillotta-et-al-2016) (2016, eqs. 1 to 4) write it:

$$\mathbf{q} = -f\kappa_\mathrm{Sp}\nabla T, \qquad \kappa_\mathrm{Sp} = 1.84 \times 10^{-5}\,\frac{T^{5/2}}{\ln\Psi}\ \mathrm{erg\,s^{-1}\,K^{-1}\,cm^{-1}},$$

with $\ln\Psi = 29.7 + \ln[(T_\mathrm{e}/10^6\,\mathrm{K})/(n_\mathrm{e}/\mathrm{cm^{-3}})^{1/2}]$ for the temperature $T_\mathrm{e}$ and density $n_\mathrm{e}$ of the electrons. The factor $f \le 1$ stands for a tangled field, which binds the electrons to its lines: a tangled field lowers the conductivity 100 to 1000 times ([Chandran and Cowley](#chandran-cowley-1998) 1998), turbulence over many scales to at most $f \approx 0.2$ ([Narayan and Medvedev](#narayan-medvedev-2001) 2001). Armillotta et al. take $f = 0.1$, and [Kooij, Grønnow and Fraternali](#kooij-et-al-2021) (2021), who let only free electrons conduct, find 0.03 to 0.15 for the most realistic orientations of the field by calibrating against anisotropic conduction in MHD ([topic 34, §2.7](34-cold-clouds-and-mixing-layers.md#27-clouds-with-fields-and-anisotropic-conduction)). **[GK]** This reduction holds for a single scale of coherence.

### 2.2 Saturation

The classical flux assumes a mean free path of the electrons, $\lambda_\mathrm{e} \approx 10^4\,T^2/n_\mathrm{e}$ cm, much shorter than the scale $L_T = T/|\nabla T|$ of the temperature ([Cowie and McKee](#cowie-mckee-1977) 1977, eqs. 3 to 6). They bound it by the flux of a Maxwellian gas facing a cold absorber, reduced by 0.4 for the electric field that stops the current (eqs. 7 and 8):

$$q_\mathrm{sat} = 0.4\left(\frac{2k_\mathrm{B}T_\mathrm{e}}{\pi m_\mathrm{e}}\right)^{1/2}n_\mathrm{e}k_\mathrm{B}T_\mathrm{e} = 5\Phi_\mathrm{s}\rho c_\mathrm{iso}^3,$$

with the electron mass $m_\mathrm{e}$, $c_\mathrm{iso}^2 = p/\rho$ the isothermal sound speed and $\Phi_\mathrm{s} \approx 1.1$ for equal temperatures of electrons and ions, lower with turbulence or an oblique field. The ratio of the classical to the saturated flux, $\sigma$ in the references and $\sigma_\mathrm{sat}$ here beside the diffusion number of [topic 16, §2.1](16-diffusion.md#21-explicit-differencing), is $4.6\lambda_\mathrm{e}/(\Phi_\mathrm{s}L_T)$ for $T_\mathrm{e} = T_\mathrm{i}$ and helium as a tenth of the particles (eqs. 9 and 10). A cloud of radius $R$ in hot gas of temperature $T_\mathrm{h}$ and density $n_\mathrm{h}$ evaporates in the saturated regime where the global parameter $\sigma_0 = (T_\mathrm{h}/1.54 \times 10^7\,\mathrm{K})^2/(n_\mathrm{h}R_\mathrm{pc}\Phi_\mathrm{s})$ exceeds about 1, with $n_\mathrm{h}$ in $\mathrm{cm^{-3}}$, $R_\mathrm{pc}$ in parsecs and $\ln\Psi = 30$ (eqs. 31 and 32). Numerical calculations commonly join the regimes smoothly.

1. Form the classical flux $\mathbf{q}_\mathrm{cl} = -f\kappa_\mathrm{Sp}\nabla T$.
2. Form the saturated flux $q_\mathrm{sat} = 5\Phi_\mathrm{s}\rho c_\mathrm{iso}^3$. **[GK]** PLUTO takes it upwind of the face ([Mignone et al.](#mignone-et-al-2012) 2012).
3. Combine them by the harmonic mean $\mathbf{q} = \mathbf{q}_\mathrm{cl}/(1 + |\mathbf{q}_\mathrm{cl}|/q_\mathrm{sat})$ of [Balbus and McKee](#balbus-mckee-1982) (1982), as AthenaPK ([Brüggen, Scannapieco and Grete](#bruggen-et-al-2023) 2023, eq. 15) does, or with the ratio of the unreduced flux, $\mathbf{q} = \mathbf{q}_\mathrm{cl}/(1 + \sigma_\mathrm{sat})$, as Athena in 2016 (Armillotta et al. 2016, eqs. 6 to 8) and PLUTO (Kooij et al. 2021, eqs. 3, 6 and 7) do, or by $q_\mathrm{sat}[1 - \exp(-|\mathbf{q}_\mathrm{cl}|/q_\mathrm{sat})]$ after [Slavin and Cox](#slavin-cox-1992) (1992), as [Vieser and Hensler](#vieser-hensler-2007a) (2007a) and [Sander and Hensler](#sander-hensler-2023) (2023) do.

Armillotta et al. reduce the saturated flux for magnetic fields by $\Phi_\mathrm{s} = \sqrt f$, after the projections of Cowie and McKee onto a field oblique to the gradient, whereas Brüggen et al. leave it unreduced. **[GK]** A ratio formed from $\kappa_\mathrm{Sp}$ rather than $f\kappa_\mathrm{Sp}$, as in eq. 7 of 2016 and in the PLUTO of Kooij et al., saturates the flux at $5f\Phi_\mathrm{s}\rho c_\mathrm{iso}^3$, reduced by $f^{3/2}$ instead of $\sqrt f$. At the commit that Brüggen et al. name, AthenaPK takes the minimum of the two fluxes, with a saturated flux about 17 times weaker than that of their eq. 3; which version their runs used remains open.

### 2.3 Anisotropic conduction

Where the mean free path far exceeds the gyroradius, heat flows along the field lines ([Sharma and Hammett](#sharma-hammett-2007) 2007, eqs. 1 and 2):

$$\mathbf{q} = -\kappa_\parallel\,\mathbf{b}\,(\mathbf{b}\cdot\nabla T) - \kappa_\perp\left[\nabla T - \mathbf{b}\,(\mathbf{b}\cdot\nabla T)\right],$$

with the conductivities $\kappa_\parallel$ along and $\kappa_\perp$ across the field and $\mathbf{b} = \mathbf{B}/|\mathbf{B}|$, and in circumgalactic gas $\kappa_\perp/\kappa_\parallel \lesssim 10^{-9}$, so that $\kappa_\perp$ is set to zero (Kooij et al. 2021). Conduction changes the characteristic structure of MHD and is split from the upwind scheme, after [Parrish and Stone](#parrish-stone-2005) (2005). With $T$ at the centres and $\mathbf{b}$ and $\mathbf{q}$ on the faces, the flux along $x$ has a normal term in $\partial T/\partial x$, centred on the face, and a transverse term in $\partial T/\partial y$, interpolated to it. Centred interpolations, asymmetric or symmetric after [Günter et al.](#gunter-et-al-2005) (2005), can carry heat from cold to hot, the asymmetric scheme through its transverse term and the symmetric one also through the normal gradient that it interpolates to the corners, and drive temperatures negative at sharp gradients, so that Sharma and Hammett limit the interpolated gradients as reconstruction limits a slope ([topic 6, §2.2](06-reconstruction-and-limiting.md#22-slope-limiters)). Their limited asymmetric scheme forms the flux on the face $(i + 1/2, j)$ in five steps (eqs. 3, 5 to 8, 17 and 18).

1. Difference the normal gradient, $(T_{i+1,j} - T_{i,j})/\Delta x$.
2. Difference the transverse gradients $\partial_yT$ on the four faces $(i, j \pm 1/2)$ and $(i + 1, j \pm 1/2)$.
3. Limit them to $\overline{\partial_yT} = L\{L[\partial_yT_{i,j-1/2}, \partial_yT_{i,j+1/2}], L[\partial_yT_{i+1,j-1/2}, \partial_yT_{i+1,j+1/2}]\}$, with the monotonised central limiter $L(g_1, g_2) = \mathrm{minmod}[2\,\mathrm{minmod}(g_1, g_2), (g_1 + g_2)/2]$, that of van Leer or minmod.
4. Average $b_y$ over the four adjacent faces, and take the harmonic mean of the conductivities of the two cells beside the face.
5. Form $q_{x,i+1/2,j} = -\overline{\kappa_\parallel}\,b_x(b_x\,\partial_xT + \overline{b_y}\,\overline{\partial_yT})$, and update $\rho e$ by the divergence of the fluxes.

At an extremum the differences on opposite faces differ in sign, so that the limited transverse gradients vanish and only the normal terms, which point down the gradient, remain: no maximum grows, no minimum falls, and the temperature stays positive. The harmonic mean keeps heat out of rarefied gas, whose small heat capacity would let its temperature change rapidly. The alternatives limit both gradients of the symmetric scheme, or reduce the transverse terms where $-\mathbf{q}\cdot\nabla T$ turns negative. **[GK]** The C version of Athena uses the limited asymmetric scheme with the limiter of van Leer and AthenaPK with MC, both with arithmetic means on the faces, and PLUTO offers the limiting as an option.

### 2.4 Semi-implicit integration

Vieser and Hensler (2007a, App. A) weight an implicit update of the temperature by $\alpha$ and an explicit one by $1 - \alpha$, set by the ratio $\beta = \tau_\mathrm{hydro}/\tau_\mathrm{cond}$ of the hydrodynamic to the conductive step (eqs. A.4 to A.8):

$$\alpha = \frac{1 - \beta - \exp(-\beta)}{\beta\,[\exp(-\beta) - 1]}, \qquad \frac{1}{2} \le \alpha \le 1,$$

close to Crank–Nicolson where conduction is slow and to backward Euler where it is fast ([topic 16, §2.2](16-diffusion.md#22-implicit-differencing)). They solve the rows of their cylindrical grid as tridiagonal systems, couple the directions by fractional steps and iterate the conductivity to convergence. Armillotta et al. (2016, App. A) replace the explicit module of Athena by such sweeps.

1. Compute $\beta$ and $\alpha$ (eq. A6).
2. Form the coefficient $f\kappa_\mathrm{Sp}/(1 + \sigma_\mathrm{sat})$, with $\sigma_\mathrm{sat}$ from the centred gradient along the sweep (eq. A7).
3. Write the implicit part in $\zeta = \int\kappa_\mathrm{Sp}\,\mathrm{d}T = (2/7)\kappa_\mathrm{Sp}T$, in which the flux is linear, linearised as $\zeta^{n+1} = \zeta^n + \kappa_\mathrm{Sp}(T^n)(T^{n+1} - T^n)$ (eqs. A3 and A4).
4. Solve the tridiagonal system of each row for the temperatures after half a step along $x$ (eqs. A2 and A5).
5. Repeat along $y$ over the second half of the step.

Sander and Hensler (2023) sweep by Crank–Nicolson in Flash, alternating the order of the directions. **[GK]** The weight reproduces exactly the decay of a single mode of time scale $\tau_\mathrm{cond}$ over one step, and rises from 1/2 to 1 as $\beta$ grows, so that its limits only guard the formula. The closed form of $\zeta$ holds only approximately for the saturated coefficient, which scales as $T^{3/2}/|\nabla T|$.

### 2.5 Explicit integration and super time-stepping

The schemes of Sharma and Hammett (2007, eqs. 3 and 4) are conservative and explicit, with the step

$$\Delta t \le \frac{\min(\Delta x^2, \Delta y^2)}{2(\mathcal{D}_\parallel + \mathcal{D}_\perp)}$$

for the diffusivities of the temperature along and across the field, which an arithmetic mean on the faces would tighten by about half the ratio of the adjacent densities. Brüggen et al. (2023) split conduction from the MHD in AthenaPK and integrate it by the RKL2 super time-stepping of [Meyer, Balsara and Aslam](#meyer-et-al-2014) (2014), two super steps per update ([topic 16, §2.5](16-diffusion.md#25-super-time-stepping)). **[GK]** A diffusivity is $(\gamma - 1)\kappa/(n_\mathrm{tot}k_\mathrm{B})$ for a conductivity $\kappa$ and the density $n_\mathrm{tot}$ of particles, which the units of Sharma and Hammett, with $\gamma = 2$, write as $\kappa/n_\mathrm{tot}$, and for $\kappa_\perp = 0$, with a uniform field and density on square cells, the bound is the exact limit of stability of the asymmetric scheme, while the symmetric one, which cannot diffuse the chessboard, stays stable up to a step longer by $1/\max(b_x^2, b_y^2)$.

Detail: [Conduction and cooling 2.4.4](../references/conduction-and-cooling-synthesis.md#244-the-explicit-integration-of-anisotropic-conduction), [Conduction and cooling 2.4.5](../references/conduction-and-cooling-synthesis.md#245-super-time-stepping-of-conduction).

## 3. Tests

Armillotta et al. (2016, App. A) propagate a plane conduction front through a static medium of $10^{-3}\,\mathrm{cm^{-3}}$ on 500 cells of 2 pc for 50 Myr, against the self-similar solution of [Reale](#reale-1995) (1995), $T = T_\mathrm{c}(1 - x^2/x_\mathrm{f}^2)^{2/5}$, for the central temperature $T_\mathrm{c} \propto t^{-2/9}$ and the position $x_\mathrm{f} \propto t^{2/9}$ of the front (eqs. A8a to A8c), and Vieser and Hensler (2007a) a spherical front on a cylindrical grid. **[GK]** The plane test exercises only the classical conductivity.

The evaporation of a spherical cloud tests saturation. Cowie and McKee (1977, eqs. 21, 60 to 63) derive the classical rate $16\pi\mu\kappa_\mathrm{h}R/(25k_\mathrm{B})$, with $\kappa_\mathrm{h}$ the conductivity of the hot gas and $\mu$ the mean mass per particle, and the saturated rate $4\pi R^2\rho_\mathrm{h}c_\mathrm{iso,h}\Phi_\mathrm{s}F(\sigma_0)$, with $F = 2.73\sigma_0^{3/8}$ for $\Phi_\mathrm{s} = 1$. [Dalton and Balbus](#dalton-balbus-1993) (1993) solve the problem with the harmonic mean in closed form, as a benchmark, and Vieser and Hensler (2007a) simulate clouds of 41 pc with $\sigma_0 = 4.88$, with classical and saturated fluxes, self-gravity, heating and cooling.

A hot patch diffusing along circular field lines, after [Parrish and Stone](#parrish-stone-2005) (2005), should leave its ring uniform, which Sharma and Hammett (2007) test on $50^2$ to $400^2$ cells, also in a background a hundred times colder. A source that drives the lowest eigenmode of the temperature along closed field lines, after [Sovinec et al.](#sovinec-et-al-2004) (2004), measures the perpendicular numerical diffusion in a steady state.

## 4. Assessment

The plane front agrees with the solution of Reale within 0.01 per cent in its hottest region until it reaches the boundary, and the spherical front of Vieser and Hensler within 2 to 3 per cent in its hottest region, more at its edge. The harmonic mean lowers the conductivity locally by up to half against the zones of Cowie and McKee, and matches their profiles from $\sigma_0 \approx 35$. The classical cloud of Vieser and Hensler evaporates at 80 per cent of the analytic rate, unchanged on finer grids, but with saturation its evaporated gas mixes into a layer unstable to Rayleigh–Taylor modes and leaves at a fortieth of the saturated rate of Cowie and McKee, as the local parameter reaches 100; with heating and cooling it condenses hot gas, as the resting clouds of Sander and Hensler do. In the ring the centred schemes undershoot, below zero with the cold background, while the limited ones never do. On $400^2$ cells the perpendicular numerical diffusion is $8.4 \times 10^{-6}$ of the parallel one for the symmetric scheme, $6.5 \times 10^{-5}$ with MC, $6.8 \times 10^{-4}$ for the asymmetric scheme with MC and $1.2 \times 10^{-2}$ with minmod. In the steady state of Sovinec et al. it grows with $\kappa_\parallel$ in all schemes but the symmetric one, and in the ring in all of them, which makes the limited schemes unsuited at a reasonable resolution to anisotropies far above $10^4$, and Sharma and Hammett recommend the limited scheme with MC. **[GK]** The production runs need isotropic conduction with saturation, as in 2016, and anisotropic conduction once MHD enters them. The forms of saturation differ by an order of magnitude, through $f^{3/2}$ against $\sqrt f$ or the weaker flux of AthenaPK, more than the uncertainty of $\Phi_\mathrm{s}$, and no reference verifies a saturated flux numerically against the solution of Dalton and Balbus. The semi-implicit sweeps lift the limit of the step at the price of directional splitting, a linearised coefficient and first order where $\alpha$ nears 1, while super time-stepping keeps explicit, conservative fluxes at about the square root of the ratio of the steps ([topic 16, §2.5](16-diffusion.md#25-super-time-stepping)); both vectorise in NumPy. Open are the form of saturation and of $\sigma_\mathrm{sat}$, the integrator and its coupling to the hydrodynamics ([topic 18, §4](18-operator-splitting-and-coupling-of-processes.md#4-assessment)), and the limiter of anisotropic conduction.
