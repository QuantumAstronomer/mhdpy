# Plan of the overview of the layers

This file records how the overview of the layers (§2.3) is written with the author: what the overview is, the way of working, the steps with their status, and the decisions taken in each step. Section signs refer to CLAUDE.md. The briefs of the layers, which the overview orders, are not opened here.

## The overview

The overview sets out the layers of the architecture of mhdpy and what each of them holds. It covers the whole scope of §1.2 from the start, since briefs of higher layers can change what a lower layer must provide (§2.3). For each layer it names the briefs that the layer needs and the order in which they are opened, as the layers are built one at a time: the briefs of a layer, then its requirements, then its code, before the briefs of the next. It draws on the syntheses in `design/references/`, on the topic summaries in `design/topics/`, above all those on the methods (1 to 27) and on the software architecture (35 to 42), and on the templates in `design/templates/`.

Two rules bear on its form. Every architectural choice is made in a decision brief, which is frozen once decided (§2.4), yet the overview must take up what later briefs change. The overview is therefore a living document rather than a brief, and makes no architectural choice of its own. It is kept in `design/overview.md`, and this plan and any diagrams in the folder `design/overview/`. Once it is written, the goals, scope and success criteria of §1.1 to §1.3 become its first part, while the priorities of §1.4 stay in CLAUDE.md.

## Way of working

Each step is agreed with the author before the next begins (§2.2). Each decision is put to the author as a question with a recommendation and its reason, one decision to a question and two or three questions to a round, all on the same part (§2.1). The structure of the overview is shown as an outline before any part is written, and the cut into layers before any layer is written in full. Each part is drafted in `.scratch/overview/`, where the author reads it in full, and nothing is written into the repository before the author has agreed it. Where a part would need an architectural choice, the choice is named and left to a brief rather than made, and no brief of any layer is opened. Each step ends in a commit of its own, which also updates the table below, and every commit and every push needs the author's approval.

## Steps

1. Way of working. This plan, the form of the overview and where it lives, and where the goals, scope and success criteria go.
2. Use. The author is asked how the overview will be read and consulted: at the start of a layer, while a brief is written, and when a brief changes what a layer must provide.
3. Practice. Established practice for layered architectures and their documents is summarised, marked **[GK]**, with what suits mhdpy and what does not.
4. Structure. The overview is shown as an outline, its parts with one line each, and the parts are then decided one at a time.
5. Cut into layers. The layers are shown as an outline, one line to a layer, drawn from the topics, and the cut is decided before any layer is written in full.
6. Contents. Each stratum is written in a step of its own, 6a to 6e, from infrastructure upwards: what it holds, what it must provide to the strata above, the briefs that it needs in their order, the tests that close it, and the entries of the glossary for the terms that it introduces. Step 6f writes the remaining parts: the head, the context of a run, one time step and the crosscutting concepts.
7. Goals. The goals, scope and success criteria of §1.1 to §1.3 become the first part of the overview. CLAUDE.md keeps in §1 the priorities and a line that links the overview, and the nine citations of §1.1 to §1.3 in seven files are pointed at the overview.
8. Consistency. The overview is read together with the templates and CLAUDE.md, every topic and every module of §1.2 is checked to have its layer, and the briefs of the first layer are listed.

## Decisions

Step 1. The author decided that the overview is a living document rather than a brief. Its cut into layers is agreed as the order of work, as the cut into topics was. Every architectural choice that the cut implies is named and left to a brief, and decided sub-decisions are linked as they come. The overview lives as long as the project, as a requirement document lives as long as its component, and is updated in place, with the author's agreement, whenever a brief changes what a layer must provide. The cut is thus agreed without the form of a brief, its options and the alternatives set aside. The overview is kept in `design/overview.md`, beside the folders that it maps, so that the document cited most has the shortest path. The folder `design/overview/` of the same name holds this plan and any diagrams, as a brief keeps its diagrams beside it. Once the overview is written, the goals, scope and success criteria of §1.1 to §1.3 become its first part, since the layers answer to them. CLAUDE.md keeps in §1 the priorities with a line that links the overview, so that every session still finds them.

Step 2. The author decided that the overview is written for newcomers as well as for the author at work, so that someone new to codes of MHD can follow it without the topic summaries, as the learning goal asks for a form that others can pick up easily. Each term is therefore explained in plain words where it first appears, in a sentence, with its topic linked for the detail. The overview also shows where the work stands: each layer lists its briefs as not begun, open or decided, and its requirements and code in the same way, so that the next step of the work is read there. It runs to about 3,000 words, the goals, scope and success criteria included, so that it can be read in one sitting, with each layer in about 300 words. The explanation of terms where they first appear gave way in step 4 to a glossary, and in step 6a the limit came to hold for the prose alone.

Step 3. Established practice was summarised from Dijkstra (1968), Parnas (1972, 1979), Buschmann et al. (1996), Cockburn (2004), Nygard (2011), arc42, the C4 model and Diátaxis. The author decided that the layers are strata by kind of code, after the layers pattern of Buschmann et al.: infrastructure, mesh and data, the numerical core, physics, and the driver with the problems, each finished before the next, while step 5 sets what each stratum holds. No problem then runs before the top stratum is built, so that the lower strata are checked by tests of their own until then, and what they must provide is foreseen long before it is used, a price that the author accepted. The usable subsets of Parnas (1979), in which each layer runs problems with those below it, were set aside. The parts of the overview follow seven of the twelve optional sections of arc42: the goals, the context of a run, the layers as building blocks with what each holds and provides, a runtime view of one time step through the layers, the crosscutting concepts, the briefs as decisions with their status, and a glossary for newcomers. One diagram shows the layers with a key, as the C4 model asks. The overview is explanation in the sense of Diátaxis, saying why the layers are as they are, while the way to a first test problem belongs to the documentation of users.

Step 4. The outline follows the order of the sections of arc42: a head, the goals, the context of a run, the layers, one time step, the crosscutting concepts, the briefs and a glossary, with the sources last. The author decided that each term is explained once, in a sentence in the glossary with its topic linked, and that its first use in the text links to that entry. The briefs form part 6, after the crosscutting concepts, with a table to each stratum: its briefs in the order in which they are opened, with their subject, topics, status and what each changed in the overview, followed by a line on its requirements and code. A line at the head names the stratum in progress and its next brief. A change that a brief makes is edited into the text without marks, and git keeps the earlier wording. The briefs of every stratum are listed from the start, in a provisional order beyond the first, and a brief not yet opened is listed by its subject, as it receives its identifier only when it is opened. The diagram of the layers is an SVG that a short script beside it in `design/overview/` writes, so that a change of the strata is a change of a list in the script, with the colours of §3.3. It shows the five strata as bands, each with its components as labelled boxes, and a key, without lines of use between the components, which the briefs have yet to decide. The time step of part 4 is one of the production run, with hydrodynamics, radiative cooling, thermal conduction and a passive scalar, so that a newcomer sees where each process enters, while each choice of method is left to its brief. Which concepts part 5 names is settled with the cut in step 5, since a concept that one stratum holds is not crosscutting. The overview begins in `design/overview.md` as a skeleton of these parts, titled Overview of mhdpy, each part with a line on what it holds.

Step 5. The cut places the 42 topics in the five strata of step 3 by the kind of code that each holds, as part 3 of the overview sets out with the first pass and the extensions of each stratum. The author decided that a first pass builds the five strata for the production run, with cheap provisions for the rest of the scope, and that each later module enters the stratum of its kind of code as an extension. MHD, with constrained transport and its solvers, is the first extension, provided for in the first pass by room for face fields and an integrator chosen to serve constrained transport. Until the driver exists, the tests of the numerical core also step Sod's shock tube and a linear wave through a small loop of their own, which the driver later replaces. A brief is opened in the lowest stratum whose code its decision shapes, so that the coupling of processes is decided with the integrator. The passive scalar joins the numerical core, as its flux is the mass flux of the gas, and boundaries straddle strata 2 and 3, the characteristic conditions lying above the equations whose waves they need. Part 5 names the crosscutting concepts with the strata whose briefs settle them, and `design/overview/layers.py` draws the strata with their components into `design/overview/layers.svg`, the first pass in red and the extensions in grey. Step 6 was widened to the parts that no step had written: the five strata in steps 6a to 6e, each with its entries of the glossary, and the remaining parts in step 6f.

Step 6a. Stratum 1, infrastructure, is written in part 3 with what it provides to the strata above, the room that it leaves for extensions and the tests that close it, its briefs are listed in part 6, and its seven terms enter the glossary. The author decided the order of its six briefs: the strata in the package, the route of the kernels, execution in parallel, the package and its installation, runtime parameters and units, and testing and verification, each building on those before it. The strata in the package form a brief of their own, which makes the choices that the cut implies: the subpackages of the strata, the rule of use between them and how the rule is checked. The 3,000 words of step 2 hold for the prose that is read in one sitting, while the tables of briefs and the glossary, which are consulted rather than read through, come on top.

Step 6b. Stratum 2, mesh and data, is written in part 3 with what it provides to the numerical core and the strata above, the room that it leaves for extensions and the tests that close it. Its briefs are listed in part 6, those of the first pass and the extensions in tables of their own, and its five terms enter the glossary. The author decided that the domain in blocks forms a brief of its own beside the arrays of the state, and the order of the four briefs: the arrays of the state, the domain in blocks, the grid and its geometry, and ghost cells and boundaries. The extensions follow in a provisional order, curvilinear coordinates before mesh refinement.

Step 6c. Stratum 3, the numerical core, is written in part 3 with how a stage of the core runs, what it provides to the physics, the driver and the problem setups, its provisions for MHD and relativistic MHD and the tests that close it. Its briefs are listed in part 6, and its thirteen terms enter the glossary, linked where they first appear in parts 3 and 5. The author decided the order of its nine briefs, the integrator before the fluxes: the equations and their variables, equations of state, the integrator, the coupling of processes, source terms, reconstruction and limiting, Riemann solvers, the passive scalar and open boundaries. The extensions follow in a provisional order, MHD first, then methods of higher order, artificial viscosity and relativistic MHD. The author also placed gravity of a fixed potential and rotating frames, which the cut had left out, as an extension of stratum 4, a process added through the sources of the core, and the diagram shows it there.

Step 6d. Stratum 4, physics, is written in part 3 with what conduction and cooling do in a step, what the stratum provides to the driver and the problem setups, its provisions for further diffusion and chemical networks, and the tests that close it, each stepped through a small loop in its tests, as those of the core are. Its briefs are listed in part 6, and its four terms enter the glossary. The author decided that its first pass has two briefs, thermal conduction, drawing on topics 16 and 17, and radiative cooling, drawing on topics 19 and 20, so that the general frameworks of diffusion and of stiff sources enter as sub-decisions and wait in full for the extensions that use them, and that conduction comes first. The nine extensions follow in a provisional order, those nearest the production runs first: further diffusion with MHD, gravity of a fixed potential, elliptic solvers and self-gravity, particles, driven turbulence, the shearing box, radiation, cosmic rays and chemical networks.
