# Self-gravity

## 1. Overview

Self-gravity couples the Poisson equation $\nabla^2\Phi = 4\pi G\rho$, solved as in [topic 14](14-elliptic-solvers.md), to the flow through the sources $\rho\mathbf{g}$ in the momentum and $\rho\mathbf{v}\cdot\mathbf{g}$ in the energy, with $\mathbf{g} = -\nabla\Phi$. Three questions remain beyond the solver: when in a step the potential is solved, how the forces and the release of gravitational energy are discretised so that momentum and energy stay conserved, and how the boundaries fix the potential. Ulula solves by FFT twice per step on periodic domains, ZEUS at most once per cycle, in practice about one cycle in ten, by sparse-matrix solvers with multipole boundaries, and Athena++ by FFT or multigrid in every stage, with the source terms of [Mullen, Hanawa and Gammie](#mullen-et-al-2021) (2021). pyro offers no self-gravity, and Zingale's text holds only the outline of a chapter on it. Particle-mesh gravity and gravity on refined meshes with conservative sources are only named. The general centring of sources is treated in [topic 13, §2.1](13-source-terms.md#21-centring-in-time).

## 2. Methods

### 2.1 The solve within a step

Each code ties the potential to the density of a given moment. Ulula adds its sources in two half steps around the sweeps and solves for the potential of the current density at each, twice per step ([Diemer](#diemer-2025) 2025). ZEUS solves at the start of its source step, with the density of that time, and only where a trial potential computed from the stored one differs from it by more than about $10^{-5}$ in relative terms, which in practice needs a solution about one cycle in ten ([Stone and Norman](#stone-norman-1992a) 1992a, §4.7). Athena++ solves after every stage of its integrator, so that each stage uses the potential of its own density: once per stage with multigrid, which runs a task list of its own ([Tomida and Stone](#tomida-stone-2023) 2023, §3.2.3), and with FFT after every stage that a main stage follows. A solve per stage is not the cheapest choice, but it suits any integrator.

Periodic domains carry a uniform background without net force, so that the solvers remove the mean density from the source and fix the zero point of the potential by convention: Athena++ sets the mode of zero wavenumber to zero, Ulula sets its kernel there to one, and multigrid subtracts the averages of source and solution. Isolated systems take boundary values from a multipole expansion, in ZEUS about the origin and in Athena++ to the hexadecapole about the centre of mass ([topic 14, §2.4](14-elliptic-solvers.md#24-sparse-matrix-solvers-and-isolated-boundaries)). Ulula allows self-gravity only with periodic boundaries.

### 2.2 Conservative source terms

Momentum and energy are conserved exactly when the sources equal the divergence of a gravitational flux, a stress tensor for the momentum and an energy flux for the energy (Mullen et al. 2021, eqs. 8 to 24):

$$\rho\mathbf{g} = -\nabla\cdot\mathsf{T}_\mathrm{g}, \qquad \mathsf{T}_\mathrm{g} = \frac{1}{4\pi G}\left[\nabla\Phi\,\nabla\Phi - \tfrac{1}{2}(\nabla\Phi\cdot\nabla\Phi)\,\mathsf{I}\right].$$

[Jiang et al.](#jiang-et-al-2013) (2013) difference these fluxes directly. A common discretisation of the tensor, used by Athena and by Athena++ up to version 19.0, takes the tangential gravities as means of four neighbouring normal ones and squares them, which is of second order but yields a gravity with curl, spurious near a body of mass $M$ and size $L$ wherever the surrounding density falls below about $(M/L^3)(\Delta x/L)^2$. Mullen et al. (2021) evaluate the sources instead from the gravities on the faces, which the discrete Poisson equation contains. A step of VL2 runs as follows.

1. With the potential $\Phi^{(0)}$ at the start, advance the conserved variables by their fluxes over the half step.
2. Solve the Poisson equation for the new density $\rho^{(1)}$.
3. Add $\tfrac{1}{2}\Delta t\,\rho^{(0)}g_{x,i}$ to the momentum, with the cell gravity the mean of the face gravities $g_{x,i\pm1/2} = \mp(\Phi_{i\pm1} - \Phi_i)/\Delta x$ of $\Phi^{(0)}$, and alike along $y$ and $z$.
4. Add to the energy $\tfrac{1}{2}\Delta t$ times half the sum, over the six faces, of the mass flux of the update through the face times the face gravity averaged over the half step, $\tfrac{1}{2}[\mathbf{g}^{(0)} + \mathbf{g}^{(1)}]$.
5. Repeat over the full step $\Delta t$ with the fluxes, density and potential of the half step, solving for $\rho^{(2)}$ and averaging $\mathbf{g}^{(0)}$ and $\mathbf{g}^{(2)}$ in the energy.

The momentum source then equals the divergence of a discrete tensor that keeps the gravity free of curl, and the energy source the release of $\rho\Phi$ by the mass fluxes. Both conserve to round-off if the Poisson equation is solved to round-off, as FFT does. With the residual of multigrid, a uniform correction $\mathbf{g}_\mathrm{corr} = \sum\Delta\rho\,\mathbf{g}/\sum\rho$, with $\Delta\rho$ the mismatch between the cell density and the density that the discrete Poisson equation implies, restores the conservation of momentum, and energy needs exact potentials only at the ends of a step. The copy of Athena++ adds the momentum source in this form, but takes the energy source from the gravity of the stage without the average in time, so that its comment calls it not conservative. Ulula counts the potential energy in its total energy and adds $\rho$ times the change of the potential since the last solve ([topic 13, §2.2](13-source-terms.md#22-gravity)). The constraint $\nabla\times\mathbf{g} = 0$ mirrors $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{B} = 0$ of constrained transport, with curl and divergence exchanged, as Mullen et al. note.

## 3. Tests

The Jeans wave, a small sinusoidal perturbation of a uniform gas under self-gravity, oscillates for wavelengths below the Jeans length and grows above it, by the dispersion relation $\omega^2 = c_\mathrm{s}^2\kappa^2 - 4\pi G\rho_0$, for the angular frequency $\omega$ and the wavenumber $\kappa$ of a perturbation of the uniform density $\rho_0$. Athena++ runs it oblique to a periodic box with FFT and multigrid, stable on one, two and four processes, whose errors must stay below $10^{-7}$ and agree between the numbers of processes, and unstable for one e-folding time on two grids, whose errors must fall at second order, and Ulula as a collapse in one dimension. Mullen et al. (2021) follow the unstable wave into sheets and filaments to test conservation.

ZEUS lets a homogeneous sphere collapse without pressure, with the exact force of the sphere, so that every shell follows the analytic solution of [Hunter](#hunter-1962) (1962) and all reach the centre at the free-fall time, $[3\pi/(32G\rho_0)]^{1/2}$ for the initial uniform density $\rho_0$. The run ends just before, when the density has grown by almost three orders of magnitude, and so tests the radial advection under gravity.

Mullen et al. (2021) advect Spitzer sheets, polytropic equilibria of contrast near $10^4$, across a periodic domain, and hold a polytrope, $p \propto \rho^\Gamma$, of $\Gamma = 2$ in an atmosphere of one per cent of its mass for ten dynamical times, which probes the spurious accelerations of the stress tensor. Ulula lets two blobs merge in a periodic square to test the symmetry, and Tomida and Stone (2023) collapse a magnetised core on an adaptive mesh to nine levels of refinement.

## 4. Assessment

The Jeans waves of Mullen et al. converge at second order with VL2, RK2 and RK3, the gravity limiting the order, and the unstable wave conserves total energy and momentum to round-off. The Spitzer sheets converge at second order with momentum conserved to round-off. Gravitational fluxes with the common tensor destroy the atmosphere of the polytrope in a pattern of fourfold symmetry, while the source terms keep the equilibrium, in longer runs for over a hundred dynamical times; with noise of $10^{-4}$ in the potential they conserve momentum only with the correction $\mathbf{g}_\mathrm{corr}$, but show no spurious motion, unlike the fluxes, and an energy source from cell values without the average in time heats the polytrope steadily. One sweep of FMG leaves enough noise in the potential that an unstable Jeans wave fragments early and loses its symmetry, which converged potentials keep (Tomida and Stone 2023). In ZEUS's collapse the density shows a spurious spike in the first few zones at the origin on a stationary grid, which a grid collapsing with the cloud substantially reduces. **[GK]** Ulula's Jeans setup takes $G$ a factor $\sqrt{4\pi}$ too large, so that its waves are stable only for nominal Jeans lengths above about 1.9 wavelengths, and still grow fast at the 1.5 that its docstring gives for stability. Self-gravity puts the conservation of the whole scheme to a hard test: forces from face gravities free of curl cost little and remove spurious flows at large contrasts of density, while energy from mass fluxes needs a solve between the update of the density and the source. The solve per stage dominates the cost of gravity. Multigrid is the cheaper solver at large sizes and suits refined meshes, while FFT solves the discrete equation on uniform periodic meshes to round-off, as conservation to round-off requires. Open are how tightly the solver must converge, whether the conservative energy source of Mullen et al. is worth the solve between the update of the density and the source, and how the scheme extends to refined meshes.
