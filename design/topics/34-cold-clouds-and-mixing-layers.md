# Cold clouds and mixing layers

## 1. Overview

Cold clouds in hot gas are the problems closest to the production runs, in which a fountain cloud of $10^4$ K crosses a corona of about $2 \times 10^6$ K. Without cooling, shocks and the Kelvin–Helmholtz and Rayleigh–Taylor instabilities destroy a cloud of radius $R$ and density contrast $\chi$ within a few cloud-crushing times, $t_\mathrm{cc} = \chi^{1/2}R/v_\mathrm{w}$ for a wind of speed $v_\mathrm{w}$, long before the drag time $t_\mathrm{drag} \sim \chi R/v_\mathrm{w}$ in which the wind would accelerate it (Klein, McKee and Colella 1994). With cooling, gas mixed in turbulent layers at the surface and in the wake can cool again, mainly in the tail, so that clouds above a critical radius survive and grow, while conduction and magnetic fields shape both. The collection on mixing and resolution, gathered around the review of Gronke and Schneider (2026), and the studies of fountain clouds in the collection on conduction and cooling hold the problems: the shocked cloud, the radiative mixing layer, clouds in a wind and in turbulence, shattering, and fountain clouds with conduction and fields. They run in Athena, Athena++, AthenaK, AthenaPK, Cholla, ECHO, Enzo-E, FLASH and PLUTO. They build on the shocks of [topic 31](31-shocks-in-several-dimensions.md), the instabilities of [topic 32](32-instabilities-vortices-and-convection.md), conduction in [topic 17](17-thermal-conduction.md) and cooling in [topic 20](20-radiative-cooling.md), and leave out the transport of radiation, cosmic rays, self-gravity and the orbit of a fountain cloud in the potential of its galaxy.

## 2. Problems

### 2.1 The shock–cloud problem

A planar shock of Mach $\mathcal{M}$ strikes a spherical cloud of contrast $\chi$, without cooling, conduction, fields or gravity, so that the problem depends on $\chi$ alone once the shock is strong, as the equations are invariant under $t \to t\mathcal{M}$, $\mathbf{v} \to \mathbf{v}/\mathcal{M}$ and $p \to p/\mathcal{M}^2$ ([Klein, McKee and Colella](#klein-et-al-1994) 1994, eqs. 2.1 to 2.4 and 4.3). The cloud is compressed, re-expands and fragments within a few $t_\mathrm{cc}$, which global quantities follow: radii and dispersions weighted by the mass of cloud material, the circulation, and the times of drag, destruction and mixing, the last when half the mass of the cloud lies in cells that also hold intercloud gas. Runs with $\chi$ from 3 to 400, a softer equation of state in the cloud, a cylinder and a wind without a shock make it a benchmark for more realistic calculations, which Ulula and Athena++ run in simpler forms ([topic 31, §2.7](31-shocks-in-several-dimensions.md#27-clouds-in-a-wind-or-a-shock)).

### 2.2 The radiative mixing layer

A plane shear layer between cold and hot gas in pressure balance, with radiative cooling, isolates the surface of a cloud: the Kelvin–Helmholtz instability mixes the phases, the mixed gas cools, and hot gas flows into the layer at a velocity $v_\mathrm{in}$ that sets the growth of the cold phase. [Ji, Oh and Masterson](#ji-et-al-2019) (2019) simulate it in three dimensions with fields, non-equilibrium ionisation and photoionisation, testing the model of [Begelman and Fabian](#begelman-fabian-1990) (1990), and [Fielding et al.](#fielding-et-al-2020) (2020) find a fractal cooling surface of dimension 5/2. [Tan, Oh and Gronke](#tan-et-al-2021) (2021) read it as turbulent combustion, with $v_\mathrm{in} \propto t_\mathrm{cool}^{-1/2}$ below a Damköhler number $\mathrm{Da} = t_\mathrm{mix}/t_\mathrm{cool}$ of 1, the ratio of the time $t_\mathrm{mix}$ of turbulent mixing to the cooling time $t_\mathrm{cool}$, and $v_\mathrm{in} \propto t_\mathrm{cool}^{-1/4}$ above it (eqs. 53 and 54). [Lancaster et al.](#lancaster-et-al-2026) (2026) define a turbulent Field length by $t_\mathrm{eddy}(\lambda_\mathrm{F,turb}) = t_\mathrm{cool}$, where the turnover time $t_\mathrm{eddy}$ of eddies of that size equals the cooling time, the scale that a layer must resolve to mix turbulently rather than numerically. The measures are $v_\mathrm{in}$, the total cooling, the distribution of the gas in pressure and entropy, and the columns of ions such as O VI.

### 2.3 Clouds in a radiative wind

A cloud in pressure balance meets a hot wind in a box that follows it, with cooling down to a floor near the temperature of the cloud. [Gronke and Oh](#gronke-oh-2018) (2018) find that it survives and grows where the mixed gas, at the geometric means of the temperatures and densities of the phases, cools faster than the cloud is crushed, $t_\mathrm{cool,mix} < t_\mathrm{cc}$ ([Gronke and Schneider](#gronke-schneider-2026) 2026, eq. 7), which sets a critical radius (Gronke and Oh 2018, eq. 2; Gronke and Schneider 2026, footnote 12):

$$R_\mathrm{crit} \approx 2\,\mathrm{pc}\;\frac{T_\mathrm{cl,4}^{5/2}\,\mathcal{M}_\mathrm{w}}{P_3\,\Lambda_\mathrm{mix,-21.4}}\,\frac{\chi}{100},$$

with the temperature of the cloud in units of $10^4$ K, the pressure $P_3 = n_\mathrm{tot}T/(10^3\,\mathrm{cm^{-3}\,K})$, the cooling function of the mixed gas in units of $10^{-21.4}\,\mathrm{erg\,cm^3\,s^{-1}}$ and the Mach number $\mathcal{M}_\mathrm{w}$ of the wind. [Abruzzo, Fielding and Bryan](#abruzzo-fielding-bryan-2023) (2023) replace $t_\mathrm{cc}$ by about seven shear times $R/v_\mathrm{w}$ and $t_\mathrm{cool,mix}$ by a cooling time weighted towards its minimum, and [Abruzzo, Bryan and Fielding](#abruzzo-bryan-fielding-2022) (2022) follow the distribution of the mass over pressure and entropy, the entropy being raised by shocks and mixing and lowered by cooling. [Leary, Richie and Schneider](#leary-et-al-2026) (2026) run adiabatic and radiative clouds of $\chi = 100$ in winds of 100 and $1000\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$ at 4 to 48 cells per radius, the resolutions of cosmological simulations.

### 2.4 Clouds in turbulence

[Gronke et al.](#gronke-et-al-2022) (2022) place clouds in hot gas stirred to a turbulent Mach number $\mathcal{M}_\mathrm{turb}$ instead of a wind. Clouds survive above a critical radius of about 8 pc at $\mathcal{M}_\mathrm{turb} \approx 1$, for $\chi \sim 100$, cold gas at $10^4$ K, $P_3 = 1$ and a cooling function of the mixed gas of $10^{-21.4}\,\mathrm{erg\,cm^3\,s^{-1}}$, a radius proportional to $\mathcal{M}_\mathrm{turb}\,10^{0.6\mathcal{M}_\mathrm{turb}}$ (eq. 9). The largest clouds grow exponentially as they keep fragmenting, and a surviving cloud near the threshold that grows a tail grows as a power law, at a rate that the cooling luminosity also measures (eqs. 7 and 8). The same set-up with another random seed of the stirring may grow or die, so that its outcome is studied statistically.

Detail: [Mixing and resolution 2.3.10](../references/mixing-and-resolution-synthesis.md#2310-survival-and-growth-in-turbulence), [Mixing and resolution 2.6.11](../references/mixing-and-resolution-synthesis.md#2611-convergence-in-turbulent-multiphase-media).

### 2.5 Shattering

Gas larger than $c_\mathrm{s}t_\mathrm{cool}$ cannot contract as fast as it cools, and [McCourt et al.](#mccourt-et-al-2018) (2018) argue that it splits into cloudlets of the minimum of that length, $\ell_\mathrm{shatter} \sim 0.1\,\mathrm{pc}\,(n_\mathrm{cl}/\mathrm{cm^{-3}})^{-1}$ near $10^4$ K, for the number density $n_\mathrm{cl}$ of the cold gas (eq. 1). Their two-dimensional simulations follow clouds of up to $10^4\,\ell_\mathrm{shatter}$ at 150 to 600 cells per radius, alone and as many cloudlets in a wind, and count clumps denser than half the initial density of the cloud. Clouds much larger than $\ell_\mathrm{shatter}$ thus test whether a scheme fragments the cold gas to the scale of its grid.

Detail: [Mixing and resolution 2.4.3](../references/mixing-and-resolution-synthesis.md#243-a-characteristic-scale-for-cold-gas), [Mixing and resolution 2.4.4](../references/mixing-and-resolution-synthesis.md#244-shattering-in-two-dimensional-simulations), [Mixing and resolution 2.6.8](../references/mixing-and-resolution-synthesis.md#268-resolving-shattering).

### 2.6 Fountain clouds through a corona

A cloud of $10^4$ K, rich in metals, moves at its velocity of ejection from the disc, 75 $\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$, through a static corona, without gravity and in two dimensions, so that it stands for a cylinder converted to a sphere by the length $4R/3$. [Marinacci et al.](#marinacci-et-al-2010) (2010, [2011](#marinacci-et-al-2011)) follow the cooling of its wake in ECHO, at last on $6144 \times 1024$ cells of about 2 pc, some 50 per radius, and [Armillotta, Fraternali and Marinacci](#armillotta-et-al-2016) (2016) add saturated conduction with $f = 0.1$ in Athena, for clouds of 100 pc in coronae of 1 to $8 \times 10^6$ K, with semi-permeable boundaries and cells of 1 to 4 pc. The measures are the mass of gas below $10^{4.3}$ K, which grows as coronal gas condenses in the wake, and the velocity centroid of the cold gas. [Armillotta et al.](#armillotta-et-al-2017) (2017) run twelve clouds of 50 to 500 pc at 100 to 300 $\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$ through the thinner corona far from the disc.

### 2.7 Clouds with fields and anisotropic conduction

[Kooij, Grønnow and Fraternali](#kooij-et-al-2021) (2021) run fountain clouds in three dimensions in PLUTO, refined to 32 cells of about 3 pc across a radius of 100 pc, with isotropic conduction in hydrodynamics and anisotropic conduction in MHD, to calibrate $f$ ([topic 17, §2.1](17-thermal-conduction.md#21-the-classical-flux-and-its-suppression)). [Brüggen, Scannapieco and Grete](#bruggen-et-al-2023) (2023) place a cloud of 100 pc and $\chi = 1000$ in a wind of $10^7$ K at Mach 3.54 or 1 in AthenaPK, with a weak field parallel or perpendicular to the flow, a plasma beta of 100, and conduction off, isotropic or anisotropic, at about 68 cells per radius.

## 3. Assessment

About 100 cells per radius represent the adiabatic shocked cloud adequately, with the circulation and the mixing time converged while the peak vorticity grows as $1/\Delta x$, and runs at Mach 10 and 1000 agree within 15 per cent (Klein, McKee and Colella 1994). Radiative clouds converge in their cold mass, mixing and velocity at only 8 to 16 cells per radius but not in their morphology, and less readily at Mach 6 or $\chi = 10^4$; a dimensionally split PPM grows the cold mass faster than the van Leer method with constrained transport but tends to the same curves (Abruzzo et al. 2022, 2023). Leary et al. (2026) find adiabatic clouds unconverged and not even monotonic in resolution up to 48 cells, radiative clouds in the subsonic wind agreeing from 4 cells and in the supersonic wind unconverged at 48, while ram pressure accelerates them independently of resolution for 1 to $2\,t_\mathrm{cc}$. Mixing layers cool right within a factor of 2 even at 16 cells per streamwise length, with spurious dips of pressure of up to 30 per cent, and converge for cells below $L/128$ of the streamwise length $L$ (Fielding et al. 2020). Their inflow converges without conduction once the outer scale of the turbulence is resolved (Tan, Oh and Gronke 2021), but their phase structure needs $\lambda_\mathrm{F,turb}$ resolved, which fast cooling prevents (Lancaster et al. 2026). Shattering does not converge between 150 and 600 cells per radius, its clumps tracking the cells, and growth in turbulence converges only well above the critical radius. The fountain cloud of 2016 condenses 26 per cent of its mass in 60 Myr without conduction, 18 with $f = 0.1$ and 9 with $f = 0.2$, and 30, 18, 4 and below 1 per cent in coronae of 1, 2, 4 and $8 \times 10^6$ K. Its velocity centroid agrees on cells of 1 to 4 pc and its cold mass on 1 and 2 pc within a few per cent, which may reflect conduction taking over from numerical diffusion, whereas finer grids of Marinacci et al. (2010) without conduction favour evaporation. Conduction also eases convergence for Kooij et al. and Brüggen et al. Isotropic conduction evaporates the stripped cloudlets in the former and raises an evaporative wind in the latter, where anisotropic conduction matters less than the field. **[GK]** The problems tell schemes apart mainly by their numerical diffusion at the interface, which sets the mixing: integrated rates converge at modest resolution, while morphology, the distribution of temperature and the emission of ions need the Field length or $\lambda_\mathrm{F,turb}$ resolved, so that without explicit diffusion they test robustness and diffusivity rather than convergence to a unique answer. The adiabatic shocked cloud remains the hydrodynamic benchmark with a known demand of resolution, and the fountain cloud of 2016 the reference of the production runs, whose cells of 2 pc put 50 across the radius of the cloud and resolve its Field length, stated as about 20 pc but about 37 pc by eq. 12 with the reduced conductivity, by 10 to 18 cells. A mixing layer with explicit conduction and viscosity, after the benchmark of Lecoanet et al. in [topic 32, §2.1](32-instabilities-vortices-and-convection.md#21-the-kelvinhelmholtz-instability), would add a converged test, which no reference provides. The studies define cold gas by different thresholds of density, temperature or a scalar, which hampers comparisons, and their tails need domains of $\chi^{1/2}$ to $\chi$ radii together with a frame that follows the cloud, which does not lower the bound of $\chi^{1/2}$ radii, with inflow and outflow boundaries ([topic 4, §2.2](04-boundary-conditions-and-ghost-cells.md#22-periodic-outflow-and-reflecting-boundaries), [topic 4, §2.3](04-boundary-conditions-and-ghost-cells.md#23-prescribed-values-inflow-and-hydrostatic-boundaries) and [topic 5](05-non-reflecting-boundaries.md)). Open are which problems join the suite of mhdpy, the measures of mixing, the cells per radius and per Field length, and the length of the domain or the motion of its frame.
