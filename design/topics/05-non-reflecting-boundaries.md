# Non-reflecting boundaries

## 1. Overview

A boundary that truncates an open domain should let every wave leave as though the domain went on, yet copying the last interior state reflects wherever the flow crosses the boundary subsonically ([topic 4, §2.4](04-boundary-conditions-and-ghost-cells.md#24-open-boundaries-and-characteristics)), as it does on every side of the production runs, whose cloud moves at $75\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$ through a corona with a sound speed of about $200\,\mathrm{km\,s^{-1}}$. The collection on non-reflecting boundaries, gathered around the review of Colonius (2004), sets out the counting of characteristics, absorbing conditions for oblique waves (Engquist and Majda 1977; Giles 1988), characteristic conditions with relaxation (Thompson 1987; Poinsot and Lele 1992), absorbing layers, characteristic conditions through the ghost cells of a Godunov scheme (Motheau, Almgren and Bell 2017), and MHD. CASTRO and LaRe3D use such conditions, PLUTO only in a one-dimensional test of a characteristic inflow, and of the codes of the first syntheses only ZEUS extrapolates along characteristics, for its toroidal components ([topic 4, §2.4](04-boundary-conditions-and-ghost-cells.md#24-open-boundaries-and-characteristics)). Damping as a source follows in [topic 13, §2.4](13-source-terms.md#24-heating-sponges-and-problem-sources), and the clouds that need open boundaries in [topic 34](34-cold-clouds-and-mixing-layers.md). Theory guides little where the flow at the boundary is nonlinear, as at a turbulent outflow, and leaves corners, boundaries with both inflow and outflow, and sonic points open.

## 2. Methods

### 2.1 Characteristics and the one-dimensional conditions

A boundary needs one condition for every characteristic that enters the domain ([Colonius](#colonius-2004) 2004, §4; [Poinsot and Lele](#poinsot-lele-1992) 1992, Table I). For the Euler equations in three dimensions these are five at a supersonic inflow, four at a subsonic inflow, one at a subsonic outflow and none at a supersonic outflow. Poinsot and Lele call these physical conditions, and advance the variables that no condition fixes with the conservation equations and one-sided differences, since extrapolating them adds a condition of zero gradient that over-specifies the boundary. Linearised about a uniform flow in two dimensions, at a boundary normal to $x$, the one-dimensional characteristic variables of [Giles](#giles-1988) (1988, eq. 162), numbered in his order, are

$$a_1 = -c_\mathrm{s}^2\rho' + p', \qquad a_2 = \rho c_\mathrm{s}\,v_y', \qquad a_3 = \rho c_\mathrm{s}\,v_x' + p', \qquad a_4 = -\rho c_\mathrm{s}\,v_x' + p',$$

for the entropy wave, the vorticity wave and the downstream and upstream acoustic waves, with primes for perturbations. The one-dimensional non-reflecting conditions set $a_1 = a_2 = a_3 = 0$ at a subsonic inflow and $a_4 = 0$ at a subsonic outflow, and the outgoing variables come from the interior (eqs. 164 and 165). They are always well posed, but reflect waves that meet the boundary obliquely, to first order in the ratio of the tangential wavenumber to the frequency.

### 2.2 Absorbing conditions at oblique incidence

Exact non-reflecting conditions are non-local, convolutions along the boundary and in time ([Engquist and Majda](#engquist-majda-1977) 1977). Expanding them about normal incidence gives a hierarchy of local conditions, for the wave equation $w_{tt} = w_{xx} + w_{yy}$ in $x > 0$, with the boundary at $x = 0$, $w_x - w_t = 0$, $w_{xt} - w_{tt} + \tfrac{1}{2}w_{yy} = 0$ and a third order, which reflect a wave incident at the angle $\theta$ from the normal by $|(\cos\theta - 1)/(\cos\theta + 1)|^n$ for $n = 1$, 2 and 3: 17, 3 and 0.5 per cent at $45^\circ$. Not every expansion is well posed ([Trefethen and Halpern](#trefethen-halpern-1986) 1986). For the Euler equations, Giles (1988) derives local conditions of second order, of which the outflow condition advances the incoming variable in time (eq. 174).

1. Linearise about the state at the boundary, as a mean along it.
2. Form $a_1$ to $a_4$ from the perturbations.
3. At the outflow, advance $a_4$ in time by a local equation in its derivatives and those of $a_2$ along the boundary.
4. Take the outgoing variables from the interior, and recover the state on the boundary from all four.

The conditions cause no reflection between vorticity and sound and reflect sound to second order, and Padé approximants of higher order extend them to any order ([Rowley and Colonius](#rowley-colonius-2000) 2000). Every local condition reflects waves whose group velocity runs along the boundary, and corners need compatibility conditions, for which stable treatments are known only at low orders.

### 2.3 Characteristic conditions with relaxation

[Thompson](#thompson-1987) (1987), as Colonius restates him, writes the equations at the boundary through the amplitudes $\mathcal{L}$ of the waves, each the speed of a characteristic times the normal derivative of its Riemann invariant, computes the outgoing ones from the interior by one-sided differences and prescribes the incoming ones. Poinsot and Lele (1992, eqs. 15 to 23) extend this to the Navier–Stokes equations, with the acoustic amplitudes

$$\mathcal{L}_1 = (v_x - c_\mathrm{s})\left(\frac{\partial p}{\partial x} - \rho c_\mathrm{s}\frac{\partial v_x}{\partial x}\right), \qquad \mathcal{L}_5 = (v_x + c_\mathrm{s})\left(\frac{\partial p}{\partial x} + \rho c_\mathrm{s}\frac{\partial v_x}{\partial x}\right),$$

and estimate the incoming amplitudes from locally one-dimensional inviscid relations, such as $\partial p/\partial t + \tfrac{1}{2}(\mathcal{L}_5 + \mathcal{L}_1) = 0$ (eqs. 24 to 28). Setting $\mathcal{L}_1 = 0$, which Poinsot and Lele call perfectly non-reflecting, is exact only for waves at normal incidence and lets the mean pressure drift, and imposing $p = p_\infty$ reflects every plane wave, so that their outflow relaxes towards a pressure at infinity (eq. 40). At a subsonic outflow:

1. Compute the outgoing amplitudes $\mathcal{L}_2$ to $\mathcal{L}_5$ from the interior.
2. Set $\mathcal{L}_1 = K(p - p_\infty)$, with $K = \sigma_\mathrm{P}(1 - \mathcal{M}^2)c_\mathrm{s}/L$ for the largest Mach number $\mathcal{M}$, a length $L$ of the domain and a constant $\sigma_\mathrm{P}$.
3. Advance the boundary with the full conservation equations, in which the amplitudes replace the normal derivatives while the transverse and viscous terms stay.

[Selle, Nicoud and Poinsot](#selle-et-al-2004) (2004, eqs. 15 to 26) show that this outlet reflects a wave of angular frequency $\omega$ by $R = -1/[1 - 2\mathrm{i}\omega/K]$, a low-pass filter with the cut-off $K/(4\pi)$, which should lie below the lowest acoustic mode of the domain; for a duct they recommend $0.2 < \sigma_\mathrm{P} < \pi$. Poinsot and Lele used $\sigma_\mathrm{P} = 0.25$.

### 2.4 Absorbing layers

Where the flow at the boundary is nonlinear, layers next to it make disturbances decay before they reach a simple closing condition ([Colonius](#colonius-2004) 2004, §5). The simplest adds a friction, $\partial\mathbf{U}/\partial t + N(\mathbf{U}) = -\sigma_\mathrm{d}(x)(\mathbf{U} - \mathbf{U}_\mathrm{o})$ (eq. 15), with $N$ the other, possibly nonlinear terms of the equations, a damping rate $\sigma_\mathrm{d}$ and a target state $\mathbf{U}_\mathrm{o}$, after [Israeli and Orszag](#israeli-orszag-1981) (1981).

1. Choose a target state $\mathbf{U}_\mathrm{o}$ and a profile $\sigma_\mathrm{d}(x)$ that rises smoothly from zero at the inner edge of the layer.
2. Relax the state towards $\mathbf{U}_\mathrm{o}$ at the rate $\sigma_\mathrm{d}$ in each step.
3. Close the outer edge by a one-dimensional characteristic condition.

The inner edge reflects unless the damping rises slowly, so that such layers are thick. The perfectly matched layer keeps the modes of the linear equations but makes them decay, while nonlinear flow is served by thicker friction layers, artificial supersonic convection, grid stretching with filtering, or the supergrid model of [Colonius and Ran](#colonius-ran-2002) (2002), whose width and slope need little tuning. Simulations of discs damp density and velocity towards a state in rings next to the boundary at the orbital rate, without conserving mass ([de Val-Borro et al.](#de-val-borro-et-al-2006) 2006, eq. 10).

### 2.5 Characteristic conditions through ghost cells

[Motheau, Almgren and Bell](#motheau-et-al-2017) (2017) set the conditions of Poinsot and Lele through the ghost cells of the unsplit Godunov scheme of CASTRO, with PPM and four ghost cells, so that the scheme runs unchanged up to the boundary (eqs. 26 to 46).

1. Compute the outgoing amplitudes in the last interior cell by one-sided differences of second order.
2. Model the incoming ones: $\mathcal{L} = K(p - p_\mathrm{t}) - (1 - \beta)T$ at an outflow, with a target pressure $p_\mathrm{t}$, the transverse terms $T$ and a weight $\beta$, and relaxations of the velocities and the temperature towards targets at an inflow.
3. Convert the amplitudes into normal derivatives of the primitive variables, $\partial\mathbf{W}/\partial x = \mathsf{R}\mathsf{\Lambda}^{-1}\boldsymbol{\mathcal{L}}$.
4. Fill the first ghost cell by $\mathbf{W}_{i-1} = \mathbf{W}_{i+1} - 2\Delta x\,\partial\mathbf{W}/\partial x$, and the others by wider differences for the same derivative.

Corners couple the waves along both axes, and where an inflow meets an outflow the incoming wave of the outflow is set to zero. [Tarr et al.](#tarr-et-al-2024) (2024) instead build a finite-volume code in characteristic form, CHAR, that couples to another code through overlapping ghost cells.

### 2.6 Magnetohydrodynamics

With $B_x$ kept as a variable, the waves normal to a boundary travel at $v_x$, twice, $v_x \mp c_\mathrm{A}$, $v_x \mp c_\mathrm{slow}$ and $v_x \mp c_\mathrm{fast}$, so that from none to eight modes enter as the normal velocity passes these speeds (Tarr et al. 2024, eqs. 5 to 7, Table 1). The number changes along a boundary and in time, and conditions set on primitive variables can over-determine the boundary, which conditions on the incoming characteristics avoid. [Kee et al.](#kee-et-al-2025) (2025) compare two choices in LaRe3D: fixed conditions hold the incoming derivatives at their initial values, zero in all their runs, and cancellation conditions let them cancel the transverse and inhomogeneous terms.

## 3. Tests

Engquist and Majda (1977, §4) send an expanding circular wave into a boundary of a scheme of second order for the wave equation, and a wave into a subsonic inflow of the linearised shallow-water equations. Colonius (2004, fig. 2) compares the reflection coefficients of local conditions against the angle of incidence for a base flow of half the sound speed.

A vortex carried out through an outflow measures nonlinear reflection ([Colonius, Lele and Moin](#colonius-et-al-1993) 1993). Motheau et al. (2017) run one, a channel whose inflow velocity doubles, a circular pulse leaving through four outflows and a backward-facing step against ghost cells that copy the last interior value. Poinsot and Lele (1992) compare their outlets on a Poiseuille flow at a Reynolds number of 15 and Mach 0.1 in a duct, and Selle et al. (2004) a pulse of pressure in a duct for several $\sigma_\mathrm{P}$.

Kee et al. (2025) carry a spheromak out at four speeds, from faster than all modes to slower than the slow mode, against a domain that extends beyond the boundary. [Mandal et al.](#mandal-et-al-2024) (2024) drive a subsonic wind into clouds through ghost cells that hold its density, velocity and pressure in PLUTO, and test a characteristic inflow in one dimension. De Val-Borro et al. (2006) compare codes on a planet that opens a gap in a disc between damping zones.

## 4. Assessment

The first condition of Engquist and Majda reflects about 5 per cent of the circular wave and the second less than 1 per cent, against complete reflection by Dirichlet and Neumann conditions, and the Padé condition of degrees (4, 4) keeps reflections below 1 per cent up to $100^\circ$ at the outflow and $45^\circ$ at the inflow, against $20^\circ$ and $5^\circ$ for the one-dimensional conditions. Once a vortex induces a sizeable fraction of the flow speed, the linear outflow of Giles reflects it nonlinearly as much as a modified condition of Giles, which turns outgoing vorticity into sound, does linearly, while stretching with filtering lowers the reflection by orders of magnitude and a supergrid layer of 10 points to a thousandth of the vortex. The relaxed outlet gives the Poiseuille flow a pressure gradient 0.998 times the exact one where extrapolation distorts the outlet, and in the duct $\sigma_\mathrm{P} = \pi$ clears all modes while $10\pi$ keeps the lowest three. Copied ghost cells settle the channel in a wrong state, distort the vortex and square the pulse, which characteristic ghost cells avoid. In MHD both conditions agree with the larger domain to about 1 per cent of a standard deviation while the outflow exceeds the Alfvén speed, and differ from it by order unity below. The over-specified wind of Mandal et al. loses 20 per cent of its density and roughly doubles its Mach number at the inflow, and their characteristic inflow keeps density and velocity but lets the pressure depart by 90 per cent. The damping zones lose 3 to 8 per cent of the mass of the disc, and four codes still reflect. **[GK]** The characteristic inflow of Mandal et al. holds $c_\mathrm{s}/\gamma - v_x$ fixed rather than the linear characteristic variable, which doubles the change of pressure, so that its departure of 90 per cent need not hold for the conditions of 2.1 and 2.5. The production runs need open boundaries in subsonic flow on every side. Their upstream and lateral boundaries see mostly sound, which characteristic ghost cells suit at little cost if they relax towards the known state of the corona: its pressure where gas leaves, and its velocity and temperature where it enters. In the frame of the corona the normal velocity on these boundaries stays near zero and changes sign, so that the number of incoming waves changes along them. The downstream boundary receives the nonlinear wake, where theory guides least and a layer or a longer domain does best. No reference measures how strongly the semi-permeable boundary of 2016, which the paper does not define and which is presumably a zero-gradient outflow, reflects in a fountain cloud, which a comparison with a larger domain, after Kee et al., would settle. Open are the condition of each side, the target state and its relaxation, the corners, and whether the frame follows the cloud.
