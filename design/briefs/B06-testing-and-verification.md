## Scope

The brief settles the tools and rules by which the tests of every stratum decide a numerical result ([§5](../overview.md#5-crosscutting-concepts) of the overview). It decides where the tools lie, the norms in which errors are measured and the variables that they take, how orders of convergence are measured and stated, which comparisons must agree exactly and what agreement to round-off means, what conservation to round-off means, how regression tests store and compare their results, how the check of requirements collects the citations of the tests, and whether the tests guard against compiled kernels older than their sources. Its tools must serve the criteria of correctness of [§1.3](../overview.md#13-success-criteria) of the overview and the tests that close stratum 1 by [§3.1](../overview.md#31-infrastructure), among them the tools of testing on sequences of known order. It chooses no problem: the problems that verify the first pass, with their measures and thresholds, are left to the brief of that name ([§6.5](../overview.md#65-driver-and-problems)), and the tests of each component to its requirements. The history by which a run checks its conservation is left to the brief on outputs, restarts and analysis, and the benchmark of the rate of cell updates stays with [B2D4](B02-kernels.md#b2d4-the-benchmark).

The brief builds on [B1D4](B01-strata-in-package.md#b1d4-the-place-of-the-tests), which places the tests in `tests/`, in folders that mirror the strata and are bound by the rule of [B1D2](B01-strata-in-package.md#b1d2-the-rule-between-the-strata), and on [B1D5](B01-strata-in-package.md#b1d5-the-check-of-the-rule), whose check runs from a test at the top of `tests/`. It builds on [B2D3](B02-kernels.md#b2d3-a-simple-version-beside-the-fast-one), which compares each compiled kernel with an independent version in NumPy and leaves the tolerance of the comparison to this brief, on [B3D3](B03-parallel-execution.md#b3d3-whether-it-enters-the-first-pass), under which any number of threads gives the serial result exactly, and on [B4D3](B04-packaging.md#b4d3-when-the-physics-is-chosen), which compiles each kernel in several versions that the tests must all cover. It also builds on [B4D2](B04-packaging.md#b4d2-the-build-backend), under which an edited kernel runs in its old compiled version until a new editable installation, unseen ([B4E1](../experiments/B04-packaging/E1-builds-of-each-backend/README.md)), on [B4D4](B04-packaging.md#b4d4-the-dependencies), under which NumPy is the only dependency at run time and the tools of development are locked, and on [B4D7](B04-packaging.md#b4d7-the-wheels-and-their-publication), under which the tests of mhdpy test every wheel on its own platform, macOS and Linux on x86-64 and ARM64. The check of requirements lists what the [rules of the requirements, §5](../templates/requirements-rules.md#5-agreement-and-change), ask of it.

## B6D1 The place of the tools

**Decision.** In the context of tools by which the tests of every stratum measure errors, orders and conservation, facing users and problems of stratum 5 that need the same measures, a module of `mhdpy.infrastructure` that takes arrays of NumPy and the volumes of the cells (option A) is chosen over helpers that only the tests import (B). This achieves usability and extensibility, as users and problems measure their errors as the tests do, accepting an interface that a change to the tools must respect. Decided on 2026-11-11, as recommended.

**Question.** The tests of every stratum measure errors, orders and conservation with the tools of this brief ([§5](../overview.md#5-crosscutting-concepts) of the overview), and [§3.1](../overview.md#31-infrastructure) counts these tools among the components of stratum 1. Whether they are code of the package or helpers that only the tests import must be settled before the first of them is written, since it fixes who can call them and whether the rule of [B1D2](B01-strata-in-package.md#b1d2-the-rule-between-the-strata) binds them as code or as tests. The check of requirements and a guard against stale kernels read the repository rather than a run, and so lie in `tests/` whatever is chosen here. To be read first are [topic 28, §2.3](../topics/28-verification-and-testing.md#23-convergence-without-an-exact-solution) and [§2.5](../topics/28-verification-and-testing.md#25-regression-tests-and-reproducibility), [pyro 3.4](../references/pyro-synthesis.md#34-output-analysis-and-testing), [Ulula 2.8.17](../references/ulula-synthesis.md#2817-conservation-and-convergence) and [Athena 3.8](../references/athena-synthesis.md#38-regression-tests).

**Option A: in the package.** The tools form a module of `mhdpy.infrastructure`, which the tests of every stratum import and users can call. pyro keeps the comparison of two outputs, by which its regression tests decide and which also runs from the command line, in a module of its package, `pyro/util/compare.py`, and Ulula checks conservation within the function that runs a simulation ([pyro 3.4](../references/pyro-synthesis.md#34-output-analysis-and-testing), [Ulula 2.8.17](../references/ulula-synthesis.md#2817-conservation-and-convergence)). For mhdpy, a user can measure the order of convergence of a problem of their own, or the conservation of a run, with the functions that verify the code, and the problems of stratum 5 can report their errors through them. The tools fall under the rule and the check of the code without further configuration, and as stratum 1 knows nothing of grids, they take arrays of NumPy and the volumes of the cells rather than the objects of the mesh. They join the interface that users see, and need tests of their own in `tests/infrastructure/`, as §3.1 of the overview asks.

**Option B: helpers of the tests.** The tools form a module within `tests/`, which the tests of every stratum import but which the wheel does not hold. Athena++ keeps its norms and its comparison of quantities on different grids beside its regression tests, apart from its code ([Athena 3.8](../references/athena-synthesis.md#38-regression-tests)). For mhdpy, the package holds only what runs a simulation, and the tools can change without regard to users. The module lies in `tests/infrastructure/`, from which the tests of every stratum may import under [B1D4](B01-strata-in-package.md#b1d4-the-place-of-the-tests), once `tests/` is a package, as [B1D5](B01-strata-in-package.md#b1d5-the-check-of-the-rule) foresees. Users cannot call the tools, so that a problem of stratum 5 that reports its error through the driver must compute it apart, and the tools still need tests of their own.

**Experiments.** None, since the options differ in who can call the tools, which no measurement decides.

**Recommendation.** Option A, since the functions that verify mhdpy then also serve the problems of stratum 5 and the studies of convergence and checks of conservation of its users. A newcomer who runs a standard problem, as [§1.3](../overview.md#13-success-criteria) of the overview foresees, can then measure its error as the tests do. The tools cost one module of NumPy in the wheel, under the rule and the check of the code. Where the tools lie changes neither the results nor the speed of a run. The option serves usability and extensibility, the fourth priority of §1, and gives up none above it, at the price of an interface that a change to the tools must respect.

**Set aside.** Option B would return if the tools came to need a package beyond NumPy, since helpers of the tests may use the locked tools of development, while the package may not ([B4D4](B04-packaging.md#b4d4-the-dependencies)).

## B6D2 The norms

**Decision.** In the context of errors measured against known solutions, smooth or with shocks, facing norms that fall at different orders across a jump, the $L_1$ norm of each conserved variable weighted by volume, combined by the root of the sum of the squares, with the maximum errors beside it (option A), is chosen over the $L_2$ and maximum norms of each variable (B) and the fractional $L_1$ norm of one variable (C). This achieves correctness, as one norm serves smooth problems and shock tubes and compares with Athena++, accepting thresholds set in the code units of each problem. Decided on 2026-11-11, as recommended.

**Question.** The criteria of correctness of [§1.3](../overview.md#13-success-criteria) of the overview compare smooth problems and problems with discontinuities with known solutions, and each requirement on an error or an order must name the norm in which it is measured and the variables that it takes, which [topic 28, §4](../topics/28-verification-and-testing.md#4-assessment) leaves open. The norm must be settled before the tools of [B6D1](#b6d1-the-place-of-the-tools) are written, since it fixes the thresholds that the brief on the problems that verify the first pass will set, and whether they can be set against those of Athena++. To be read first are [topic 28, §2.2](../topics/28-verification-and-testing.md#22-norms-and-the-order-of-convergence) and [§3](../topics/28-verification-and-testing.md#3-tests), [Zingale 2.1.6](../references/computational-hydrodynamics-synthesis.md#216-norms) and [2.3.6](../references/computational-hydrodynamics-synthesis.md#236-errors-and-convergence), and [Athena 2.16.1](../references/athena-synthesis.md#2161-linear-waves) and [2.16.4](../references/athena-synthesis.md#2164-shock-tubes).

**Option A: $L_1$ of the conserved variables, after Athena++.** The error of each conserved variable is the $L_1$ norm of its difference from the reference, $\sum_i |q_i - q_i^\mathrm{ref}| V_i / \sum_i V_i$, and the error of the state is the root of the sum of the squares of these errors, which Athena++ reports as their root mean square. Beside them stand the maximum error of each variable and the largest ratio of maximum to $L_1$ error. Athena++ measures its linear waves and shock tubes so, and its regression tests bound these numbers, while Ulula, too, measures its convergence by an $L_1$ norm ([Athena 2.16.1](../references/athena-synthesis.md#2161-linear-waves), [2.16.4](../references/athena-synthesis.md#2164-shock-tubes), [topic 28, §3](../topics/28-verification-and-testing.md#3-tests)). **[GK]** A jump smeared over a few cells adds to a normalised $L_p$ norm in proportion to $\Delta x^{1/p}$, so that across a shock the $L_1$ norm falls at first order, the $L_2$ norm at order one half and the maximum norm not at all. For mhdpy, one norm serves the smooth problems and the shock tubes of every stratum, and its errors compare with those that Athena++ publishes and with the thresholds of its tests. The ratio of maximum to $L_1$ error shows a fault confined to a few cells, such as a wrong copy of ghost cells between the blocks of [B3D2](B03-parallel-execution.md#b3d2-the-share-of-each-worker), which a global norm averages away. The sum over variables mixes quantities of different dimensions, which the code units of [B5D4](B05-parameters-and-units.md#b5d4-the-system-of-units) keep near unity.

**Option B: $L_2$ and maximum norms of each variable, after Zingale's text.** Each variable is measured by the normalised $L_2$ norm of its difference from the reference, absolute and relative to the norm of the reference, and by its maximum norm. Zingale's text measures the error of advection after one period so, and pyro's scripts tabulate the $L_2$ and maximum norms of every variable ([Zingale 2.1.6](../references/computational-hydrodynamics-synthesis.md#216-norms), [2.3.6](../references/computational-hydrodynamics-synthesis.md#236-errors-and-convergence), [pyro 3.4](../references/pyro-synthesis.md#34-output-analysis-and-testing)). For mhdpy, each variable is judged apart, and a relative error needs no scale of the problem. Across a shock, however, these norms fall at order one half or not at all, so that shock tubes need a norm of their own, and the relative error of a small wave on a uniform state measures the wave against the state rather than against its amplitude.

**Option C: $L_1$ of the fractional difference of one variable, after Ulula.** The error is the $L_1$ norm of $|q_i - q_i^\mathrm{ref}|/|q_i^\mathrm{ref}|$ for one variable chosen for each problem, such as the density. Ulula measures its three problems of convergence so ([Ulula 2.8.17](../references/ulula-synthesis.md#2817-conservation-and-convergence)). For mhdpy, the error needs no scale and weighs every cell alike, which suits a positive variable such as the density or the temperature. A variable that passes through zero, such as a velocity, has no fractional error, and a fault in a variable not chosen goes unseen.

**Experiments.** None, since the references and the behaviour of each norm at a jump settle what each option measures.

**Recommendation.** Option A, since the $L_1$ norm falls at the order of the scheme on smooth problems and at first order across a shock, where the norms of option B fall at half that order or not at all, so that one norm serves every problem of the first pass. Its errors compare with those of Athena++, whose solutions are trusted, and the maximum beside it shows faults confined to a few cells. A requirement may still name a single variable where a problem concerns one, such as the temperature of a conduction front. The option serves correctness, the first priority of §1, and gives up the errors free of scale of options B and C, as its thresholds are set in the code units of each problem.

**Set aside.** Option C would return for a positive quantity that spans orders of magnitude, such as the density of a cloud in a hot corona, since a fractional error weighs the cells of the cloud and of the corona alike.

## B6D3 The order of convergence

**Decision.** In the context of orders that must match the formal order of the scheme, facing faults that keep the order but enlarge the error, the order of the finest pair of grids bounded from below and its error from above, the tools giving the order of every pair (option B), is chosen over a range on the order alone (A) and the slope of a fit (C). This achieves correctness, as a fault shows whether it lowers the order or enlarges the error, accepting that an order above the formal one passes. Decided on 2026-11-11, as recommended.

**Question.** [§1.3](../overview.md#13-success-criteria) of the overview asks that the measured order of convergence on smooth problems match the formal order of the scheme, and [§3.1](../overview.md#31-infrastructure) tests the tools that measure it on sequences of known order. How the tools take an order from the errors of [B6D2](#b6d2-the-norms), and what a requirement on convergence bounds, must be settled before the first of them is written. The errors are measured against a known solution or, where none is known, against the grid twice as fine averaged onto the coarser one, the finer grid of one pair serving as the coarser of the next ([topic 28, §2.3](../topics/28-verification-and-testing.md#23-convergence-without-an-exact-solution)). How a known solution is sampled, at the centres of the cells or as their averages, is left to each problem. Athena++ samples at the centres, corrects to cell averages for its methods of fourth order, and requires convergence to continue until round-off or the steepening of a wave of finite amplitude stops it, so that the finest grid must lie above both ([Athena 2.16.1](../references/athena-synthesis.md#2161-linear-waves)). To be read first are [topic 28, §2.2](../topics/28-verification-and-testing.md#22-norms-and-the-order-of-convergence), [§3](../topics/28-verification-and-testing.md#3-tests) and [§4](../topics/28-verification-and-testing.md#4-assessment), and [pyro 3.4](../references/pyro-synthesis.md#34-output-analysis-and-testing).

**Option A: the order of the finest pair within a range.** For each pair of grids that differ by a factor of two, the tools give the order $r = \log_2(\epsilon_N/\epsilon_{2N})$, and a requirement bounds that of the finest pair from below and from above, as the [rules of the requirements, §3](../templates/requirements-rules.md#3-the-requirements), exemplify with an order between 1.9 and 2.1. Zingale's text and pyro's scripts take the order between successive pairs ([Zingale 2.3.6](../references/computational-hydrodynamics-synthesis.md#236-errors-and-convergence), [pyro 3.4](../references/pyro-synthesis.md#34-output-analysis-and-testing)). For mhdpy, the statement follows the criterion of §1.3 to the letter, as an order above the range fails as one below it does. The upper bound catches an error that stems from something other than the scheme, such as a reference that shares it, but also fails where an error falls faster than the formal order before it reaches it. Neither bound sees a fault that keeps the order but enlarges the error, as one that doubles the diffusion of the scheme would.

**Option B: the order from below and the error from above, after Athena++.** The tools give the order of each pair as in option A, and a requirement bounds the order of the finest pair from below and the error on the finest grid from above. Athena++ bounds its linear waves so, by errors below $3.7 \times 10^{-8}$ and ratios of the errors of two grids of at most 0.325, an order of at least 1.62, its shock tubes by an error of at most 0.011 and a ratio of at most 0.6, and its diffusion by orders above 1.99 ([Athena 2.16.1](../references/athena-synthesis.md#2161-linear-waves), [2.16.4](../references/athena-synthesis.md#2164-shock-tubes), [2.11.1](../references/athena-synthesis.md#2111-viscosity-thermal-conduction-and-non-ideal-mhd)). For mhdpy, a fault that lowers the order fails as in option A, and one that keeps the order but enlarges the error fails at the bound on the error, which the brief on the problems can set against the errors of Athena++, measured in the same norm by B6D2. An order above the formal one passes.

**Option C: the slope of a fit within a range.** The tools fit a line to $\log\epsilon$ against $\log\Delta x$ over all grids of a sequence, and a requirement bounds its slope from below and from above. No reference fits so. **[GK]** For mhdpy, a pair whose error is uneven, as when a feature lies differently against a coarse grid, weighs less in the slope. But the coarse grids, whose errors still hold terms of higher order, pull the slope away from the formal order, and the slope changes with the grids that a sequence holds.

**Experiments.** None, since the options differ in what a requirement bounds, which the references and the definitions settle.

**Recommendation.** Option B, since a bound on the error beside the order catches the faults that keep the order but enlarge the error, which a range on the order alone lets pass in options A and C, and its thresholds can be set against those of Athena++, whose norm B6D2 adopts. The finest pair gives the order nearest the asymptotic one, which a fit mixes with that of the coarse grids, while the tools still give the order of every pair, so that a test can show its trend. The option serves correctness, the first priority of §1, and gives up the upper bound on the order of option A, as an order above the formal one points to the test rather than to the scheme.

**Set aside.** The upper bound of option A would return if orders above the formal one came to hide faults of the tests, such as references that share the error of the scheme. Option C would return for problems whose errors scatter from pair to pair, as when a feature lies differently against each grid, since a fit weighs each pair less.

## B6D4 Exact and round-off comparisons

**Decision.** In the context of tests that compare two results of one computation, facing compilers that fuse products and sums on ARM64 and references written apart from their loops, exact agreement for the same code and, for different code, differences below $2^{12}\epsilon$ of the largest magnitude of each variable in the state, over at most 64 steps (option B), is chosen over exact agreement everywhere (A) and tolerances of NumPy for each test (C). This achieves correctness on every platform by one rule, accepting that a change of round-off within a kernel passes unseen. Decided on 2026-11-11, as recommended.

**Question.** Tests compare two results of one computation: a compiled kernel with its independent version in NumPy ([B2D3](B02-kernels.md#b2d3-a-simple-version-beside-the-fast-one)), the compiled versions of a kernel ([B4D3](B04-packaging.md#b4d3-when-the-physics-is-chosen)), runs on several threads with the serial run, which [B3D3](B03-parallel-execution.md#b3d3-whether-it-enters-the-first-pass) requires to agree exactly, a restart with the run that it continues, which [§3.5](../overview.md#35-driver-and-problems) of the overview requires to agree bit for bit, and a problem with its mirror image, which Zingale's text expects to agree to round-off and Athena++ to every digit ([topic 30, §2.2](../topics/30-shock-tubes-and-interacting-blast-waves.md#22-sods-shock-tube), [Athena 2.16.1](../references/athena-synthesis.md#2161-linear-waves)). Which comparisons must agree exactly, and what round-off means for the others, must be settled before the first kernel meets its reference, on every platform on which [B4D7](B04-packaging.md#b4d7-the-wheels-and-their-publication) tests the wheels. To be read first are [topic 28, §2.1](../topics/28-verification-and-testing.md#21-round-off-and-truncation-error) and [§4](../topics/28-verification-and-testing.md#4-assessment), and [Performance in Python 3.9](../references/performance-in-python-synthesis.md#39-pace-components-compiled-at-initialisation) and [3.10](../references/performance-in-python-synthesis.md#310-veros-backends-behind-one-code).

**Option A: exact everywhere.** Every comparison requires the two results to be equal, element by element, so that a test catches any change of the arithmetic. **[GK]** A kernel and its reference then need the same operations in the same order, the same mathematical functions, and no product and sum fused into one rounding. Compilers for ARM64 fuse them by default, clang within a statement and GCC across statements, while the baseline of x86-64 lacks the instruction. For mhdpy, the build must switch fusion off on every platform, and each reference must follow the order of its loop rather than the formulas of its topic, against the independence that B2D3 asks of it.

**Option B: exact for the same code, one rule for different code.** Results of the same compiled code on the same data agree exactly: runs on any number of threads, restarts, and mirror images where the scheme is written symmetrically. Results of different code, a kernel and its reference, the versions of a kernel, or its source run uncompiled, agree where the largest difference of each variable stays below $k\epsilon$ times its largest magnitude, the measure of B2E1, with one $k$ for all tests and any NaN failing. Pace checked its port to a relative difference of $10^{-14}$, and Veros its routines after one step to $10^{-6}$ ([Performance in Python 3.9](../references/performance-in-python-synthesis.md#39-pace-components-compiled-at-initialisation), [3.10](../references/performance-in-python-synthesis.md#310-veros-backends-behind-one-code)). For mhdpy, a reference follows its topic and the compilers their defaults, while a fault of the algorithm, which differs by a truncation error, still fails. A change of round-off within a kernel passes unseen.

**Option C: exact for the same code, tolerances of NumPy for different code.** As option B, but each test compares different code by `numpy.testing.assert_allclose`, with a relative and an absolute tolerance of its own, as pyro compares its benchmarks by `numpy.allclose` ([pyro 3.4](../references/pyro-synthesis.md#34-output-analysis-and-testing)). **[GK]** The function accepts each element within the relative tolerance of its value plus the absolute one, and takes two NaN as equal unless told otherwise. For mhdpy, each test fits its tolerances to its problem, but an element near zero, such as a velocity at rest, needs an absolute tolerance, which compares small values more loosely than large ones ([topic 28, §4](../topics/28-verification-and-testing.md#4-assessment)). No rule holds across the tests.

**Experiments.** [B6E1](../experiments/B06-testing-and-verification/E1-round-off-against-references/README.md) measures how far round-off moves the stage of [B2E2](../experiments/B02-kernels/E2-declarations-for-cython/README.md) from plain NumPy when it is compiled with the fusion of clang or of GCC on ARM64, or written apart from its loop. Without fusion, the stage equals plain NumPy exactly over 64 steps. Fusion and the second reference each move the state by at most $1.25\epsilon$ of the largest value of $\rho$, $m_x$ and $E$ after one step and $8\epsilon$ after 64, and $m_y$, whose largest value is small beside the pressure in its flux, by $61\epsilon$ and $597\epsilon$. Against its own largest value, the change of one stage differs by up to $5.8 \times 10^4\,\epsilon$, a measure that grows with the number of cells.

**Recommendation.** Option B, since fusion on ARM64 and a reference written apart from its loop each break exact agreement by B6E1, which option A could keep only by switching fusion off everywhere and tying each reference to the order of its loop. Against the largest magnitude of each variable in the state, round-off stays below $600\epsilon$ over 64 steps, so that $k = 2^{12}$, about $9 \times 10^{-13}$, leaves a margin, **[GK]** as NumPy and the C library compute functions such as `exp` and `pow` differently in their last bits, while a fault of the algorithm differs by orders of magnitude more. A change is measured against the state that it changes, since against its own size its round-off grows with the number of cells, and a comparison of different code spans at most 64 steps. Option C would need two tolerances in every test. The option serves correctness, the first priority of §1, on every platform, and readability, the third, by one rule for all tests, and gives up the exact check of a kernel against its reference.

**Set aside.** Option A would return if a fault came to hide within the tolerance, since without fusion and in one order of operations a kernel and its reference agree exactly, as B6E1 shows. Option C would return for a quantity that spans many orders of magnitude, such as a cooling rate, whose small values one scale per variable compares only loosely.

## B6D5 Conservation

**Decision.** In the context of totals that must be conserved to round-off over whole runs, facing round-off that grows with the steps and totals that vanish, a bound $|Q_n - Q_0| \le 16\epsilon n S$, with $S$ the larger sum of the magnitudes of the cells at the start or the end and the cells added by `math.fsum` (option B), is chosen over the relative change of each total (A) and conservation by construction alone (C). This achieves correctness, as every total is checked on any grid and over any number of steps, accepting a worst case far above the typical change. Decided on 2026-11-11, as recommended.

**Question.** [§1.3](../overview.md#13-success-criteria) of the overview asks that conserved quantities be conserved to round-off wherever the boundaries permit, and [§3.3](../overview.md#33-numerical-core) closes the numerical core with a linear wave that conserves mass, momentum and energy to round-off on a periodic domain. What to round-off means for a total over the domain, $Q = \sum_i q_i V_i$, must be settled with the tools of [B6D1](#b6d1-the-place-of-the-tools), since every requirement on conservation names a tolerance. The rule of [B6D4](#b6d4-exact-and-round-off-comparisons) compares two results over at most 64 steps, while a total must hold over a whole run, on any grid, also where it vanishes, as the momentum of a wave at rest does. Where boundaries or sources add to a total, the rule applies to the change less what they added, which the history of the brief on outputs is to record. To be read first are [topic 28, §2.4](../topics/28-verification-and-testing.md#24-conservation-resolution-and-the-design-of-suites), [Ulula 2.8.17](../references/ulula-synthesis.md#2817-conservation-and-convergence) and [3.3](../references/ulula-synthesis.md#33-the-runtime-function), [ZEUS 2.1.2](../references/zeus-synthesis.md#212-operator-splitting) and [Athena 2.8.4](../references/athena-synthesis.md#284-finite-volumes-and-geometric-source-terms).

**Option A: the relative change of each total, after Ulula.** A test requires $|Q_n - Q_0| / |Q_0|$ after $n$ steps to stay below a fixed tolerance, and leaves out a total that starts near zero. Ulula reports this change for mass, energy and each momentum whose initial sum exceeds $10^{-6}$ in code units, as a vanishing total gives no measure ([Ulula 3.3](../references/ulula-synthesis.md#33-the-runtime-function)). For mhdpy, the measure reads as a fraction of the total. A fixed tolerance, however, holds for runs of some length only, as round-off accumulates with the steps, and a total that should stay at zero, such as the momentum of a symmetric problem, escapes the check, although a fault that moves it from zero is what the check should catch.

**Option B: a bound of round-off on the steps and the magnitudes.** A test requires $|Q_n - Q_0| \le k\epsilon n S$, where $S$, the larger of $\sum_i |q_i| V_i$ at the start and at the end, is positive also where the total vanishes, and the tools add the cells by `math.fsum`. **[GK]** That function avoids the loss of precision of a running sum ([Python](#python-math)). Each addition rounds its result by at most $\epsilon/2$ of its value, and a scheme adds to each cell a few times a step, three times in Heun's method, so that round-off alone changes a total by at most a small multiple of $\epsilon n S$ ([Higham](#higham-2002) 2002, §2.2, §4.2), while its typical change grows only as the square root of the number of roundings. For mhdpy, every total is checked, zero or not, on any grid and over any number of steps, by a tolerance that follows from the arithmetic rather than from a choice for each test. With $k = 16$ the bound holds for integrators that round each cell up to some thirty times a step. Being a worst case, it lies far above the typical change, so that a fault smaller than it passes.

**Option C: by construction, after ZEUS and Athena++.** No test checks a total: conservation follows from updates written as differences of fluxes that neighbouring cells share. ZEUS keeps the total of each advected quantity constant to round-off by conservative differencing, and Athena++ discretises its geometric sources so that it conserves angular momentum to machine precision ([ZEUS 2.1.2](../references/zeus-synthesis.md#212-operator-splitting), [Athena 2.8.4](../references/athena-synthesis.md#284-finite-volumes-and-geometric-source-terms)). For mhdpy, no tolerance is needed. A fault that breaks the sharing of a flux, such as stale ghost cells between two blocks of [B3D2](B03-parallel-execution.md#b3d2-the-share-of-each-worker), or a source written in a form that does not conserve, then passes unseen, while §1.3 asks that conservation be shown rather than assumed.

**Experiments.** None, since the rounding of floating-point arithmetic bounds what round-off can do to a total, and the references show what each option checks.

**Recommendation.** Option B, since it checks every total, also one that vanishes, which option A leaves out, and grows with the steps as round-off does, which a fixed tolerance cannot, from a bound that holds by the arithmetic rather than by a choice for each test. A fault that breaks conservation, such as a face between two blocks whose fluxes the blocks compute from different ghost cells, changes a total at every step by far more than $16\epsilon n S$, and fails where option C would let it pass. The option serves correctness, the first priority of §1, and gives up a tolerance near the typical change, as the bound is a worst case.

**Set aside.** Option A would return in the reports of a run, where the relative change of a total, as Ulula prints it, reads at a glance, while the tests keep the bound.
