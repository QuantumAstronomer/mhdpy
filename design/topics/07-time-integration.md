# Time integration

## 1. Overview

The conservative update of [topic 3](03-grids-and-finite-volume-discretisation.md) (section 2.3) needs fluxes averaged over a time step. Time integration supplies them in one of two ways. A single-step scheme predicts the face states at the half step from the equations themselves, by characteristic tracing or the Hancock step, and solves the Riemann problems once per step. The method of lines evaluates the fluxes at one time and integrates the resulting ordinary differential equations with a Runge–Kutta method. In several dimensions, either the directions are swept in turn, by dimensional splitting, or all are updated at once, which the corner transport upwind (CTU) method achieves in a single step. ZEUS and Ulula split by direction, pyro offers CTU, the method of lines and spectral deferred corrections, Athena uses CTU and the van Leer integrator, and Athena++ the method of lines alone. Constrained transport within the integrators follows in topic 10, schemes of fourth order in topic 8, and the splitting of physical processes in topic 16. Implicit integration of the hydrodynamics is only named.

## 2. Methods

### 2.1 Stability and the Courant condition

Explicit methods evaluate the right-hand side with known values, as Euler's method $\mathbf{y}^{n+1} = \mathbf{y}^n + \Delta t\,\mathbf{f}(t^n, \mathbf{y}^n)$ does, and implicit ones with the new values, at the cost of a Newton iteration that suits stiff problems ([Zingale's text](#zingale-2025), eqs. 1.20 to 1.38). For the advection of $u$ at a velocity $v > 0$, the upwind method $u^{n+1}_i = u^n_i - \mathcal{C}(u^n_i - u^n_{i-1})$, with $\mathcal{C} = v\,\Delta t/\Delta x$, takes the difference on the side from which information arrives (eqs. 4.2 to 4.15). A Fourier mode of phase $\kappa\,\Delta x$ per cell is amplified per step by

$$|A|^2_\text{upwind} = 1 - 2\,\mathcal{C}(1 - \mathcal{C})\,(1 - \cos\kappa\Delta x), \qquad |A|^2_\text{centred} = 1 + \mathcal{C}^2\sin^2\kappa\Delta x,$$

so that upwinding is stable for $0 \le \mathcal{C} \le 1$, while the centred difference grows for every $\mathcal{C}$. The upwind method solves in truth $u_t + v\,u_x = \tfrac{1}{2}v\,\Delta x\,(1 - \mathcal{C})\,u_{xx}$, a diffusion that vanishes at $\mathcal{C} = 1$, where the data shift by exactly one cell, and turns negative beyond. A stable method must also have a numerical domain of dependence that contains the physical one, a condition necessary but not sufficient, and its step obeys the Courant–Friedrichs–Lewy (CFL) condition $\mathcal{C} \le 1$. The implicit upwind method is stable for any $\mathcal{C}$, but accuracy still needs a small $\mathcal{C}$.

### 2.2 Predictor–corrector schemes in one dimension

A scheme of second order predicts the face states at $t^{n+1/2}$ by a Taylor expansion in space and time, with the time derivative taken from the equation, and corrects with the conservative update (Zingale's text, eqs. 5.4 to 5.10). For advection the upwind face value is $u_i + \tfrac{1}{2}(1 - \mathcal{C})\,\delta u_i$, with the limited difference $\delta u_i$ of topic 5. For a system in primitive variables, $\partial\mathbf{W}/\partial t = -\mathsf{A}\,\partial\mathbf{W}/\partial x$, characteristic tracing keeps only the waves that move towards the face (eqs. 8.10 to 8.28):

$$\mathbf{W}^{n+1/2}_{\mathrm{L},i+1/2} = \mathbf{W}_i + \frac{1}{2}\sum_{m:\ \lambda^{(m)} \ge 0}\left[1 - \frac{\Delta t}{\Delta x}\lambda^{(m)}\right]\big(\mathbf{l}^{(m)}\cdot\delta\mathbf{W}_i\big)\,\mathbf{r}^{(m)},$$

with the eigensystem of cell $i$, and alike for the right state with the waves moving to the left. Athena, pyro and Zingale's text write this from a reference state, the profile reached by the fastest wave towards the face, $\mathbf{W}_i + \tfrac{1}{2}[1 - (\Delta t/\Delta x)\max(\lambda^{(M)}, 0)]\,\delta\mathbf{W}_i$, from which each slower wave removes what it cannot carry to the face ([Colella and Glaz](#colella-glaz-1985) 1985; [Stone et al.](#stone-et-al-2008) 2008, §4.2.2). With solvers of the HLL family, Athena also adds the waves moving away.

The Hancock step of Ulula advances the reconstructed states by half a step without characteristic projection ([Diemer](#diemer-2025) 2025, eqs. 24 to 27). It runs as follows.

1. Reconstruct the limited differences $\delta\mathbf{W}_i$ in primitive variables (topic 5).
2. Advance both face states of the cell by $\mathbf{W}^{n+1/2}_{\mathrm{L/R}} = \mathbf{W}_{\mathrm{L/R}} - \tfrac{1}{2}(\Delta t/\Delta x)\,\mathsf{A}(\mathbf{W}_i)\,\delta\mathbf{W}_i$, or in conserved variables by the difference of the fluxes of the two face states.
3. Solve the Riemann problems between the advanced states, and update conservatively.

With linear reconstruction this is the MUSCL–Hancock scheme ([van Leer](#van-leer-1979) 1979), which in split form is stable for $\mathcal{C} < 1$. Without any prediction, the Euler integrator of Ulula is wrong at first order in time and, with MC slopes, unstable. **[GK]** Eq. 27 of the paper prints a plus sign in step 2, where the equation needs the minus sign that the code uses.

### 2.3 Dimensional splitting

A split method sweeps the directions in turn, each with the one-dimensional scheme and from the state that the previous sweep left, without transverse terms (Zingale's text, eqs. 5.43 to 5.47). Alternating the order of the sweeps from step to step, x–y and then y–x, with the same $\Delta t$ for both, gives second order ([Strang](#strang-1968) 1968). Ulula fills the ghost cells after each sweep and, as the first sweep can raise the fastest speed, accepts a larger Courant number in the second, repeating the step with a shorter one beyond a maximum. ZEUS splits its transport into sweeps that are not symmetric within a step but alternate from cycle to cycle, since [Finn and Hawley](#finn-hawley-1989) (1989) found the symmetric half–full–half sequence not worth its cost at moderate resolutions ([Stone and Norman](#stone-norman-1992a) 1992a, §4.4). ZEUS also splits the source terms from the transport (topic 16). Splitting cannot serve MHD in conservative form with constrained transport: a sweep along $x$ keeps $B_x$ fixed, while $\nabla\cdot\mathbf{B} = 0$ holds only if all components evolve together (Stone et al. 2008, §5). **[GK]** The alternation lets the leading splitting errors of consecutive steps cancel, and Strang's symmetric form achieves this within one step.

Detail: [Zingale 2.3.14](../references/computational-hydrodynamics-synthesis.md#2314-dimensional-splitting), [Ulula 2.3.2](../references/ulula-synthesis.md#232-dimensional-splitting), [Ulula 2.3.3](../references/ulula-synthesis.md#233-the-time-step), [ZEUS 2.1.2](../references/zeus-synthesis.md#212-operator-splitting), [ZEUS 2.4.1](../references/zeus-synthesis.md#241-conservative-directionally-split-advection), [Athena 2.6.3](../references/athena-synthesis.md#263-ctu-in-two-dimensions).

### 2.4 Corner transport upwind

CTU predicts each face state with the flow across its direction as well, and updates all directions at once ([Colella](#colella-1990) 1990). In two dimensions it takes four stages (Zingale's text, eqs. 8.63 to 8.78).

1. Predict the normal states $\hat{\mathbf{U}}$ on both sides of all faces, as in one dimension.
2. Solve Riemann problems between them for transverse states $\mathbf{U}^\mathrm{T}$ and fluxes.
3. Correct each normal state by the transverse flux difference of the cell it comes from, $\mathbf{U}^{n+1/2}_{\mathrm{L},i+1/2,j} = \hat{\mathbf{U}}_{\mathrm{L},i+1/2,j} - \tfrac{1}{2}(\Delta t/\Delta y)\,[\mathbf{G}(\mathbf{U}^\mathrm{T}_{i,j+1/2}) - \mathbf{G}(\mathbf{U}^\mathrm{T}_{i,j-1/2})]$, with cell $i + 1$ for the right state.
4. Solve the final Riemann problems and update conservatively.

The transverse term lets diagonal flow reach the upwind corner cell. In three dimensions the method of [Saltzman](#saltzman-1994) (1994) couples the corners with 12 Riemann solutions per cell and step and stays stable up to $\mathcal{C} = 1$, while Athena uses the variant of [Gardiner and Stone](#gardiner-stone-2008) (2008) with 6 solutions, stable for $\mathcal{C} \le 1/2$. In MHD, both need source terms that keep the field free of divergence (topic 10).

Detail: [Zingale 2.3.15](../references/computational-hydrodynamics-synthesis.md#2315-corner-transport-upwind), [Zingale 2.5.16](../references/computational-hydrodynamics-synthesis.md#2516-unsplit-methods-in-several-dimensions), [pyro 2.2.1](../references/pyro-synthesis.md#221-the-corner-transport-upwind-method), [pyro 2.4.3](../references/pyro-synthesis.md#243-sources-and-transverse-corrections), [Athena 2.6.3](../references/athena-synthesis.md#263-ctu-in-two-dimensions), [Athena 2.6.4](../references/athena-synthesis.md#264-ctu-in-three-dimensions).

### 2.5 The method of lines

The method of lines reconstructs in space alone and integrates the semi-discrete system (Zingale's text, eqs. 5.58 to 5.61, 8.119 to 8.122)

$$\frac{d\mathbf{U}_{i,j}}{dt} = \mathcal{L}(\mathbf{U}) = -\frac{\mathbf{F}_{i+1/2,j} - \mathbf{F}_{i-1/2,j}}{\Delta x} - \frac{\mathbf{G}_{i,j+1/2} - \mathbf{G}_{i,j-1/2}}{\Delta y} + \mathbf{S}_{i,j}$$

with a Runge–Kutta method, which couples the directions through its stages, needs no characteristic projection and takes in source terms naturally. Athena++ offers the predictor–corrector VL2 of [Stone and Gardiner](#stone-gardiner-2009) (2009), its default, and strong-stability-preserving methods ([Stone et al.](#stone-et-al-2020) 2020, eqs. 11 to 15):

$$\text{VL2:}\ \ \mathbf{U}^{n+1/2} = \mathbf{U}^n + \tfrac{1}{2}\Delta t\,\mathcal{L}_1(\mathbf{U}^n),\quad \mathbf{U}^{n+1} = \mathbf{U}^n + \Delta t\,\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{U}^{n+1/2});$$
$$\text{RK3:}\ \ \mathbf{U}^{(1)} = \mathbf{U}^n + \Delta t\,\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{U}^n),\quad \mathbf{U}^{(2)} = \tfrac{3}{4}\mathbf{U}^n + \tfrac{1}{4}[\mathbf{U}^{(1)} + \Delta t\,\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{U}^{(1)})],\quad \mathbf{U}^{n+1} = \tfrac{1}{3}\mathbf{U}^n + \tfrac{2}{3}[\mathbf{U}^{(2)} + \Delta t\,\mathcal{L}(\mathbf{U}^{(2)})],$$

where $\mathcal{L}_1$ uses the fluxes of first order of the donor cell. RK1, RK2, the method of Heun, a low-storage RK4 and the five-stage SSPRK(5,4) complete the set, all in the low-storage form of [Ketcheson](#ketcheson-2010) (2010). pyro integrates its method of lines with the classic RK4 by default, and with spectral deferred corrections at three Gauss–Lobatto nodes, four iterations giving fourth order at nine evaluations of $\mathcal{L}$ per step. The method of lines tolerates smaller steps in several dimensions: for upwinding with equal speeds the stable Courant number is $1/d$ in $d$ dimensions, and Athena++ caps it at 1/2 for VL2 in two and three dimensions. VL2 converges at second order with a predictor of first order, whereas RK2 needs a reconstruction of second order in both stages and proved no better ([White, Stone and Gammie](#white-et-al-2016) 2016). Athena++ chose the method of lines because the characteristic projection of CTU is hard to extend to relativity and arbitrary coordinates.

### 2.6 The time step

The step follows from the fastest signal over all cells. Split and CTU schemes take $\Delta t = \mathcal{C}\min_{i,d}\,\Delta x_d/(|v_d| + c)$ over the directions $d$, while fully unsplit schemes such as the method of lines need either the more restrictive $\Delta t = \mathcal{C}/\max_i\sum_d (|v_d| + c)/\Delta x_d$, as pyro takes, or a lower cap on $\mathcal{C}$, as Athena++ sets. A Courant number alone thus does not define a step (Zingale's text, eqs. 5.56 and 5.57). Here $c$ is the sound speed, or in MHD the fast speed. ZEUS combines the limits of sound, of the flow relative to the grid and of its artificial viscosity as $\Delta t = C_0\,[\max(\delta t_1^{-2} + \delta t_2^{-2} + \delta t_3^{-2} + \delta t_4^{-2})]^{-1/2}$ with $C_0 \approx 0.5$ (Stone and Norman 1992a, eqs. 73 to 78). The codes also bound the growth of the step: ZEUS by 30 per cent per step, Athena++ by a factor of two, and Zingale's text suggests a factor of 1.2 and a first step ten times shorter. Athena++ also respects the parabolic limits of diffusion (topic 15).

## 3. Tests

Advecting a Gaussian and a top hat with periodic boundaries at several Courant numbers shows the stability and the dissipation of each scheme, against the exact solution of a shifted profile (Zingale's text, eq. 4.6). The text runs the upwind and the centred method in one dimension, the method of lines with RK4 at $\mathcal{C} = 0.8$ and $0.4$, and CTU diagonally across a periodic grid of $32 \times 32$ cells. Ulula advects a blob diagonally across its domain with constant and linear states under the Euler integrator and the Hancock step.

## 4. Assessment

The upwind method diffuses the more the smaller $\mathcal{C}$, and the centred method grows without bound (Zingale's text). RK4 with the method of lines is unstable for the top hat at $\mathcal{C} = 0.8$ and stable at $0.4$, while CTU smears the diagonal profiles as in one dimension, with a slight undershoot behind the top hat. Ulula's blob turns unstable with MC under the Euler integrator and is sharpest with MC under the Hancock step. Gardiner and Stone (2008) find the CTU of 6 and of 12 Riemann solutions accurate and robust at about the same cost and prefer the first for its simplicity and memory, and the Hancock step in primitive variables hardly differs from that in conserved ones (Diemer 2025). **[GK]** Splitting is the simplest route to several dimensions and keeps $\mathcal{C}$ near one, but it breaks symmetries and excludes constrained transport in conservative form, which rules it out for MHD. CTU is the most accurate and stable per step, at two Riemann solutions per face, but its characteristic tracing ties it to the eigensystem and grows complex in three dimensions and with new physics. The method of lines is the most modular, with sources, diffusion and further physics entering each stage alike, at the price of smaller steps in several dimensions and a Riemann solution per face and stage. As the cost of a run is set by cell updates, what counts is the work per unit of simulated time, the Riemann solutions per cell over the stable Courant number, at the accuracy reached. Open are which integrator serves both constrained transport and later physics, whether CTU repays its complexity against VL2 or RK3, and whether implicit or super time-stepping is needed where stiff terms enter (topics 15 and 17).
