# Radiation transport

## 1. Overview

Where radiation holds a significant share of the energy and momentum of a flow, or dominates its exchange of energy, the equations of the matter gain those of the radiation, which are angular moments of the transfer equation for the specific intensity $I$. The moments form a hierarchy that needs a closure, as the equations of gas dynamics do, and an explicit step of the radiation would be limited by the crossing time of light and, in static diffusion, by the time of diffusion across a cell, both far shorter than the step that the flow allows. ZEUS-2D solves the moment equations of the comoving frame implicitly and closes them by a tensor Eddington factor from a formal solution of the static transfer equation by short characteristics ([Stone, Mihalas and Norman](#stone-et-al-1992a) 1992). Athena++ transports the intensities along a discrete set of directions, explicitly at a reduced speed of light or implicitly by an iteration of its own. The repository of Zingale's text holds a draft, which the published version leaves out, whose one worked part is an elliptic solver for grey radiation, and pyro and Ulula treat no radiation. Optically thin cooling follows in [topic 20](20-radiative-cooling.md) and the photochemistry of six rays in [topic 19, §2.4](19-reactions-chemistry-and-cooling.md#24-interstellar-chemistry-with-heating-and-cooling).

## 2. Methods

### 2.1 Moment equations and their closure

ZEUS-2D writes the moment equations in the comoving frame, in which the matter is isotropic and its interaction with radiation simplest, and keeps every term of order unity in one of three regimes (Stone, Mihalas and Norman 1992, §2). For the radiation energy density $E_\mathrm{r}$, flux $\mathbf{F}_\mathrm{r}$ and stress tensor $\mathsf{P}_\mathrm{r}$, integrated over frequency, the stress along the direction of propagation is about $E_\mathrm{r}$ and $F_\mathrm{r} \approx cE_\mathrm{r}$ in the streaming limit, where the mean free path of a photon exceeds the length scales of the system; in the static diffusion limit $\mathsf{P}_\mathrm{r} \approx E_\mathrm{r}\mathsf{I}/3$; and in the dynamic diffusion limit the moving matter carries the radiation along. The equations read (eqs. 7 and 9)

$$\rho\frac{D}{Dt}\left(\frac{E_\mathrm{r}}{\rho}\right) = -\nabla\cdot\mathbf{F}_\mathrm{r} - \nabla\mathbf{v}:\mathsf{P}_\mathrm{r} + 4\pi\kappa_\mathrm{P}B_\mathrm{P} - c\kappa_\mathrm{E}E_\mathrm{r}, \qquad \frac{\rho}{c^2}\frac{D}{Dt}\left(\frac{\mathbf{F}_\mathrm{r}}{\rho}\right) = -\nabla\cdot\mathsf{P}_\mathrm{r} - \frac{\chi_\mathrm{F}}{c}\mathbf{F}_\mathrm{r},$$

with the Planck function $B_\mathrm{P}$ and the Planck, energy and flux means $\kappa_\mathrm{P}$, $\kappa_\mathrm{E}$ and $\chi_\mathrm{F}$ of the opacity. The matter gains the force $\chi_\mathrm{F}\mathbf{F}_\mathrm{r}/c$, and its internal energy is combined with that of the radiation into one equation, which cancels the terms of their exchange and keeps the scheme accurate near radiative equilibrium. A tensor Eddington factor closes the system, $\mathsf{P}_\mathrm{r} = \mathsf{f}E_\mathrm{r}$ with $\mathsf{f} = \oint I\,\mathbf{n}\mathbf{n}\,d\omega/\oint I\,d\omega$ over the directions $\mathbf{n}$ and the solid angle $\omega$. The Eddington approximation $\mathsf{f} = \mathsf{I}/3$ of diffusion suits only optically thick flows, and flux limiters patch its failure in thin ones with fixes of unknown generality, whereas factors from a formal solution apply to thin and thick media alike. ZEUS-2D solves the moment equations with the factors of the previous step and updates them afterwards, without iteration, as the anisotropy of the radiation usually changes little within a step; this fails where the flow evolves on the time scale of the radiation, as at an ionisation front. The equations assume nonrelativistic flow, local thermodynamic equilibrium and grey radiation. **[GK]** The two later copies of ZEUS-2D contain no solver of the transfer equation and take their Eddington factors from flux limiters instead.

### 2.2 Implicit solution with automatic flux limiting

Explicit radiation would face Courant limits set by $c$ and by the diffusion coefficient $c/3\chi_\mathrm{F}$, so that ZEUS-2D keeps the hydrodynamics explicit and solves the moment equations implicitly, split like the hydrodynamics into a source and a transport step (Stone, Mihalas and Norman 1992, §3).

1. Integrate the momentum equation of the radiation analytically over the step with an integrating factor, automatic flux limiting after [Mihalas and Weaver](#mihalas-weaver-1982) (1982), $\mathbf{F}^{n+1}_\mathrm{r} = \exp(-c\chi_\mathrm{F}\Delta t)\,\mathbf{F}^n_\mathrm{r} - [1 - \exp(-c\chi_\mathrm{F}\Delta t)]\,(c/\chi_\mathrm{F})\nabla\cdot(\mathsf{f}E_\mathrm{r})$, with opacity and divergence averaged over the step.
2. Insert the flux into the energy equations of radiation and matter, centred between the old and the new time with a weight above one half on the new, which leaves two coupled, strongly nonlinear equations per zone for $e$ and $E_\mathrm{r}$. **[GK]** A weight of one half, Crank–Nicolson, which the streaming test runs, is stable too, but damps the shortest modes only slowly ([topic 16, §2.2](16-diffusion.md#22-implicit-differencing)).
3. Solve these by Newton–Raphson iteration, each iteration a sparse banded system of $2 \times 2$ blocks over nine radiation and five material energy densities, by GMRES, with the boundary conditions built into the matrix.
4. Advect the radiation variables explicitly with the mass fluxes of the hydrodynamics, directionally split, and add the terms that curvilinear coordinates give the flux.

Each Newton step may change the solution by at most about 10 per cent, and convergence to $10^{-5}$ typically takes 4 to 8 iterations; without it after 20, the step is shortened. The opacities on the faces are energy-weighted harmonic means, needed for the transport of energy across unresolved interfaces (§3.1 of the paper), although its difference formulae take the flux mean at the averaged $e$ and $\rho$ of the two adjacent zones (eqs. 108 and 109). The draft of Zingale's text solves the equation $\alpha_\mathrm{r}\psi + \nabla\cdot(\beta_\mathrm{r}\nabla\psi) + \mathbf{u}_\mathrm{r}\cdot\nabla\psi = f$ of grey radiation, for an unknown $\psi$, a source $f$ and coefficients $\alpha_\mathrm{r}$, $\beta_\mathrm{r}$ and $\mathbf{u}_\mathrm{r}$, by multigrid, citing [Zhang et al.](#zhang-et-al-2011) (2011) for its form, changing only the smoother and the residual of [topic 14, §2.3](14-elliptic-solvers.md#23-multigrid) and setting inhomogeneous boundary values through the ghost cells. **[GK]** For $c\chi_\mathrm{F}\Delta t \gg 1$ the flux of step 1 becomes the diffusion law $\mathbf{F}_\mathrm{r} = -(c/\chi_\mathrm{F})\nabla\cdot(\mathsf{f}E_\mathrm{r})$, which for $\mathsf{f} = \mathsf{I}/3$ is Fick's law, and for $c\chi_\mathrm{F}\Delta t \ll 1$ an explicit update, so that it passes smoothly between the limits.

### 2.3 The formal solution by short characteristics

For the Eddington factors, ZEUS-2D solves the transfer equation in its static form, with the fluid frozen in its momentary state, on planes tangent to the radial shells of its grid, each plane with rays at several polar angles (Stone, Mihalas and Norman 1992, §4). Long characteristics, traced from each point back to the boundary, cost $\mathcal{O}(N^3)$ on $N^2$ points, and short ones, which integrate only across the adjacent cell, $\mathcal{O}(N^2)$. Along a short characteristic from the upwind point $M$ on the edge of the cell to the point $O$, the formal solution reads (eq. 55)

$$I_O = I_M\exp(-\Delta\tau_{MO}) + \int_0^{\Delta\tau_{MO}} S(\tau)\exp[-(\Delta\tau_{MO} - \tau)]\,d\tau,$$

with the source function $S$, which may include scattering, the optical depth $\tau$ along the segment and its total $\Delta\tau_{MO}$, from an opacity whose logarithm is linear along it. The upwind intensity $I_M$ is interpolated linearly along the grid line that the ray crosses. This diffuses the field in angle, while parabolic interpolation, as by [Kunasz and Auer](#kunasz-auer-1988) (1988), diffuses less but can overshoot to negative intensities. Quadratures over the rays and planes, with weights for an intensity piecewise linear in the cosine $\mu$ of the polar angle and in that of the azimuth and checked by test integrals at the start, give the moments and so the Eddington factors. The solver exploits the symmetries of its coordinates and is restricted to Cartesian and cylindrical ones.

### 2.4 Discrete ordinates in Athena++

Athena++ evolves the specific intensities themselves along a discrete set of directions, a given number of polar cosines per octant or polar and azimuthal angles suited to spherical-polar grids, optionally in groups of frequency. The flux of the intensities splits into a part relative to the moving gas and an advective part with the velocity of the gas, with face values from a reconstruction of selectable order, and in optically thick cells a factor of the optical depth per cell reduces the dissipation of the fluxes towards the limit of diffusion. Absorption, emission and scattering, and optionally Compton scattering, act in the comoving frame: the intensities are transformed to it and back with Doppler factors, and the intensities and the temperature of the gas are solved implicitly in each cell, by iteration on the temperature. The exchanged momentum and energy enter the hydrodynamics as source terms. The explicit module adds its tasks to the main integrator, and the step then respects a speed of light that a factor may reduce. The implicit module runs an iteration of its own in each stage.

1. Sweep all blocks, each cell taking the intensities of its neighbours from the previous iterate, as in a Jacobi iteration, optionally in red and black order or with relaxation.
2. Compute fluxes, sources and boundaries in each sweep, with a task list of its own.
3. Stop when the change falls below a tolerance or after a maximum number of sweeps.
4. Add the source terms to the gas and treat Compton scattering.

Detail: [Athena 2.15.1](../references/athena-synthesis.md#2151-radiation-transport).

## 3. Tests

The moment solver of ZEUS-2D is tested in each limit (Stone, Mihalas and Norman 1992, §6). For streaming, the Eddington factor along the direction of propagation is set to one and the opacity made small, and a flux imposed at the left boundary sends a square wave of radiation energy across 100 zones at $c\Delta t/\Delta x = 2$. The step is fully implicit or by Crank–Nicolson, and the front is compared with its analytic position.

For static diffusion, a Gaussian pulse of radiation energy diffuses from a corner of a Cartesian grid of $20 \times 20$ zones with reflecting boundaries, for diffusion coefficients of about 1 and 100, over one and about a hundred diffusion times. Its amplitude follows an analytic law. For dynamic diffusion, a Gaussian pulse is advected diagonally across $64 \times 64$ zones, with a diffusion time about $10^4$ times that of advection.

The transfer solver (§7 of the paper) sends a searchlight beam through an optically thin grid without sources, of $16 \times 16$ and $64 \times 64$ zones, at polar angles of $\pm 45^\circ$, along which it should neither spread nor weaken. Source functions constant and linear along a ray, with a constant opacity, have analytic intensities that linear interpolation should reproduce. A cylinder in thermal equilibrium and fields beamed along either axis check the Eddington factors qualitatively.

Athena++ runs, with both modules, linear waves of radiation hydrodynamics, also on refined meshes, and the relaxation of gas and radiation to a common temperature, also with groups of frequency, and holds a problem generator for a beam. The waves must have errors below thresholds of order $10^{-9}$, falling to at most 0.55 times the error of the next coarser grid, and the relaxation must reach reference temperatures and energies within $10^{-5}$ to $10^{-3}$. The draft of Zingale's text tests its elliptic solver on a cosine product on the unit square with inhomogeneous Dirichlet boundaries.

## 4. Assessment

The fully implicit step gives a smooth streaming front at the speed of light, if diffusive, also at $c\Delta t/\Delta x = 4$, while Crank–Nicolson leaves oscillations in its wake that decay only slowly and would make the matter behind a front fluctuate, so that the fully implicit step is clearly better (Stone, Mihalas and Norman 1992). The diffusing pulse follows the analytic amplitude within 4 per cent on $20 \times 20$ zones and stays smooth and symmetric. The advected pulse turns slightly square through the directional splitting and loses almost half its amplitude to the diffusion of the advection on so coarse a grid, less with PPA. The searchlight beam spreads considerably on the coarse grid and much less on the fine one, symmetrically, and the linear tests err by at most 1.92 per cent, in a band along the diagonal where the slope of the intensity jumps. The Eddington factors come out exactly for the thermal field and with off-diagonal components negative over part of the domain for beamed ones. GMRES converges poorly at Courant numbers far above the explicit limit, and the sparse solves take most of the time of the module. The draft's elliptic solver converges at second order. **[GK]** The paper writes the amplitude of the diffusing Gaussian as $t^{-1/2}$, which holds in one dimension, and in two it falls as $t^{-1}$. Moment methods with an approximate closure are cheap but fail where beams cross, factors from a formal solution are accurate but need a transfer solver and, for fast changes, iteration, and discrete ordinates are the most general, at a cost that grows with the number of angles. Every implicit option needs large sparse or iterative solves, demanding in Python, and sixteen of the radiation files of Athena++, one of its boundaries and some of its problem generators carry headers of version 3 of the GNU General Public License, which matters wherever code is adapted. Radiation transport lies in the eventual scope of mhdpy but not in the production runs. Open are which closure the first radiation module uses and how its implicit solve reuses the elliptic solvers of [topic 14](14-elliptic-solvers.md).
